Routine data on preventive chemotherapy for schistosomiasis are confounded, clustered, heavy-tailed and heaped. We study how cross-fitted augmented inverse probability weighted (AIPW) estimators behave, and how they should be computed, when least squares is replaced by Student-t outcome engines: Huber and t iteratively reweighted least squares, and a two-component t scale-mixture regression fitted by an ECM algorithm, alongside a residual-clipped augmentation. We give four short propositions (exact AIPW bias, the efficiency bound, the estimand of clipping, monotone ascent of a scale-constrained ECM), a single-file Python implementation with cluster-level cross-fitting, cluster-robust standard errors and a cluster bootstrap, and a Monte Carlo study with up to 1000 replications per cell. Robust outcome engines leave AIPW unchanged (RMSE differences at most 0.002), whereas clipping cuts RMSE by 26-45% under symmetric contamination, under-covers, and, as a plasmode experiment built on real covariates and residuals shows, shifts the estimand when residual laws differ between arms. One-sided artefacts that differ between arms bias every estimator. Heaped data also break naive code: the MAD scale collapses and the mixture likelihood is unbounded, and we give simple guards. Applied to 1485 rounds in 362 health zones of the Democratic Republic of the Congo (ESPEN, 2014-2025), integrated delivery appears to raise programme coverage by 17.4 percentage points, yet epidemiological coverage differs by only 0.9: the gap comes from targeting denominators, a real differential artefact that no outcome engine can remove.
