Forest managers updating growing stock volume (GSV) estimates must decide whether existing LiDAR data calibrated with current inventory are sufficient or a new acquisition provides worthwhile improvements. We compared regional airborne laser scanning (ALS) acquired in 2023 with uncrewed aerial vehicle laser scanning (ULS) from 2026 in a mixed-species forest in north-eastern Germany. Both datasets were calibrated using the same 2026 personal laser scanning inventory within a change-of-support regression framework. We compared 3 m and 35 m grids, canopy height alone and with three structural metrics, and models with and without spatial random effects. Evaluation comprised 10-fold cross-validation (CV) on 118 undisturbed plots (no tree removal), prediction on 33 disturbed plots (detected removal) excluded from calibration, and CV across all 151 plots. In undisturbed CV, the lowest root mean squared prediction errors (RMSPEs) were 74.1 m3 ha-1 for ULS and 81.5 m3 ha-1 for ALS, a 9.1% reduction for ULS. Both used spatial effects and 3 m predictors, with structural metrics for ULS and canopy height only for ALS. At 3 m, canopy-height-only models performed similarly across sources, whereas adding structural metrics improved ULS predictions. Finer grids improved both sources, and spatial effects gave only small gains. On the disturbed holdout, 3 m ULS models had little bias, whereas ALS overpredicted volume, consistent with predictors representing pre-removal conditions. Canopy-height-only ULS outperformed the four-predictor models on these plots. Across all 151 plots, the 3 m four-predictor ULS models achieved an RMSPE of 70.5 m3 ha-1 versus 81.5 m3 ha-1 for the best ALS model, a 13.5% reduction. Existing ALS therefore retained substantial predictive value on undisturbed plots, while ULS provided additional information and supported estimation across both disturbance classes.
