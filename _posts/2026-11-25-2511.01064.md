Variational inference (VI) approximates a target density $p$ by the best match $q$ in a family $\mathcal Q$ of tractable distributions. The variational approximation is found by minimizing a divergence, most commonly the reverse Kullback-Leibler divergence $\text{KL}(q||p)$, although other divergences can be considered. In practice, $p \notin \mathcal Q$ and so the optimal variational approximation is misspecified. Despite this misspecification, it was recently shown that the minimizer of $\text{KL}(q||p)$ is guaranteed to recover the mean in the presence of even symmetry and likewise the correlation matrix in the presence of elliptical symmetry for a log-concave target $p$. We extend this result in several directions. First, we relax the assumption of log-concavity and instead merely require $p$ to be unimodal, allowing for heavy-tailed targets. Second, we obtain guarantees for cases where $p$ exhibits symmetry along some but not all of its coordinates. These partial symmetries arise naturally in Bayesian hierarchical models, where the prior induces a challenging geometry but still possesses axes of symmetry. Third, we extend these guarantees to $f$-divergences, a broad class which includes the forward and reverse-KL divergences and the $\alpha$-divergences.
