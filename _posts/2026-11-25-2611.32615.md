This article develops a unified partial identification approach for the average treatment effect (ATE) and the local average treatment effect (LATE) in randomized experiments with imperfect compliance, incorporating the cross-world correlation between potential treatment statuses. Even under random assignment and exclusion restriction, the correlation is unidentified. However, given the two identified marginals, it uniquely determines the relative proportions of the four principal strata, with monotonicity corresponding to a special case. We derive explicit sharp bounds for both the ATE and LATE at a fixed correlation and extend the results to prespecified correlation ranges, providing a sensitivity analysis that incorporates substantive information about compliance behavior. For the ATE, the lower bound decreases and the upper bound increases with the correlation. Although imposing monotonicity does not tighten the conventional Balke--Pearl bounds, we show that restricting the correlation can yield tighter bounds, even when defiers remain possible. For the LATE, the sharp bounds may not vary monotonically with the correlation, and we establish necessary and sufficient conditions for point identification of the LATE at any feasible correlation value, with the conventional Wald ratio identification result under monotonicity as a special case. In addition, we derive a breakdown point for the correlation that determines when the Wald ratio falls outside the sharp identified set for the LATE. Applications to four empirical studies illustrate how cross-world correlation restrictions tighten the sharp bounds for the ATE and LATE and assess the breakdown of the Wald ratio.
