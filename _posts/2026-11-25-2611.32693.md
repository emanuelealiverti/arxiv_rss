Model-based clustering of longitudinal functional data must account both for heterogeneity among curves and for temporal dependence in their latent group memberships. Functional mixture models typically treat curves as exchangeable, while temporal approaches may restrict regime persistence or ignore state-specific functional variation. We propose hidden semi-Markov models for ordered sequences of multivariate functional observations in which each latent regime has a parsimonious, state-specific functional principal subspace, extending functional high-dimensional data clustering (funHDDC) to longitudinal settings. Gaussian, contaminated-Gaussian and multivariate-$t$ emissions accommodate atypical observations, and explicit state-specific sojourn distributions govern regime persistence, with hidden Markov models as the geometric special case. Estimation uses expectation-maximization (EM) and expectation/conditional-maximization (ECM) algorithms with segmental forward-backward recursions, and identifiability is established up to label permutations and spectral indeterminacies. In simulations, temporal dependence yields the largest gains when single curves are weakly informative; with robust emissions, state-specific subspaces outperform a global functional principal component representation; and explicit durations add smaller gains when regimes overlap and sojourns are non-geometric. Applications to air-quality profiles and smartphone activity recordings, evaluated on held-out years and subjects, show improved predictive fit and illustrate how apparent duration effects can reflect time-varying transitions.
