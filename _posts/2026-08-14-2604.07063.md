Interactions and time shape many aspects of life. Everyday activities -- like conversations, emails, money transfers, citations, and even acts of violence -- are relational events: interactions between a sender and a receiver at a specific moment. At the intersection of event-history analysis and network modelling, relational event models (REMs) offer a powerful framework for studying when and why these events occur. Recent advances have made it possible to express REMs as generalized additive models, allowing researchers to capture complex, non-linear patterns over time. A hands-on guide to REMs is still missing. This tutorial fills that gap. It provides a practical introduction to REMs, incorporating the latest developments in the field. It demonstrates how to simulate synthetic relational-event data and walks through several empirical applications, comparing different modeling and inference strategies. By bringing together theory, simulation, and application, this tutorial lowers the barrier to entry and makes REMs a more accessible and practical tool.
