When independent validation data are unavailable, evaluating a fitted logistic model on new patients requires estimating prediction error and uncertainty. Evaluating the fit on training data is often optimistic. Bootstrap validation is widely used to correct this bias, but with many predictors, it can retain bias and yield unreliable confidence intervals. In this article, we study these limitations and develop single-fit estimation and inference without resampling. When the number of Gaussian predictors grows proportionally with sample size, we analyze unpenalized logistic models. We prove that standard bootstrap correction can have nonzero limiting bias even as the resample count grows and location-shifted intervals can have zero limiting coverage. Instead, we adjust the fitted scores to consistently estimate realized Brier and log losses. We account for common fitting and adjustment error to construct valid marginal intervals and joint regions. We also establish these single-fit estimation and inference guarantees for certain non-Gaussian designs in proportional dimensions. Under moderate dimension growth and possible misspecification with general predictors, we extend single-fit inference to ridge regularization and construct asymptotically valid confidence intervals for realized log loss. Simulations and analyses of hospital and diabetes datasets support our theory and demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed methods.
