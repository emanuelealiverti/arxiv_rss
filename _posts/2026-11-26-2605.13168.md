Michaelis--Menten regression remains central to enzyme-kinetic analysis, yet routine applications still often rely on homoscedastic nonlinear least squares even when assay variability increases with substrate concentration or repeated readings are clustered. We develop original-scale Michaelis--Menten estimation and inference using semiparametric score equations for independent curves and a working-covariance extension for clustered assays. In simulation, heteroscedastic modeling improved variance recovery and shortened intervals in some scenarios relative to homoscedastic nonlinear least squares, while cluster-aware profile and NLME fits improved confidence-interval coverage for the maximum reaction velocity relative to pooled analyses that ignored clustering, with interval calibration depending on the number of independent clusters . We apply the proposed method to self-driving laboratory and soil exoenzyme data. The companion R package \texttt{inferMM , available on CRAN, implements the resulting workflow for single-curve, grouped, and clustered Michaelis--Menten analysis. \texttt{inferMM supports user-defined, fixed positive variance functions for single-curve and grouped estimation and inference. For independent single curves, it also accommodates non-Gaussian errors. These results show that simple variance-function and covariance modeling can stabilize enzyme-kinetic inference when variability changes with substrate concentration or measurements are clustered.
