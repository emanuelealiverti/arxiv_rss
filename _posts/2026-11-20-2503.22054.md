tempdisagg is an open-source Python package that estimates a high-frequency time series from low-frequency benchmarks and, optionally, high-frequency indicators, such that the estimates aggregate exactly to the benchmarks. It implements the regression-based estimators of Chow-Lin, Fern\'andez and Litterman, the dynamic Chow-Lin model, the Denton and Denton-Cholette benchmarking procedures and uniform distribution, with the formulas and defaults of the R package tempdisagg, together with the autoregressive distributed-lag model of Proietti as an experimental extension. All generalised least squares estimators share one engine in which the residual covariance is applied through its triangular factor, so that one likelihood evaluation costs $O(n\,n_l^2)$ instead of $O(n^2 n_l)$. The package reports complete inference (coefficients, standard errors, likelihood-based criteria, prediction variances and a full profile of the autoregressive parameter with identification diagnostics), accepts additional exact linear constraints, multiple indicators and aggregation weights, and provides exact non-negative and cross-sectional reconciliation, rolling-origin backtesting, revision analysis, conformity diagnostics and a common-factor test. Agreement with R is enforced by an automated test suite over 29 reference cases: with a fixed autoregressive parameter predictions and statistics agree to relative differences between $10^{-15}$ and $10^{-7}$, and with an estimated parameter they agree to about $10^{-8}$ once the difference in optimiser tolerance is removed. Experiments with series whose high-frequency values are known illustrate how the reported tools support the choice of method.
