Modal regression models the conditional mode rather than the conditional mean, which is attractive for positive and right-skewed responses whose mean is distorted by the upper tail. We propose a regularized version of parametric modal regression for positive responses with high-dimensional covariates, in which the density parameters of the Gamma, Weibull and Lognormal distributions are written as functions of the conditional mode and the log-likelihood is penalized by the Lasso, adaptive Lasso or elastic-net penalties. The estimates are computed by a proximal Newton algorithm, equivalently an iteratively reweighted penalized least-squares scheme, whose inner problem is solved exactly by the coordinate-descent routines of glmnet, and the regularization parameter is selected by the extended Bayesian information criterion. We show that the Lognormal estimator coincides with the Gaussian Lasso applied to the log-transformed response, that under constant dispersion the conditional mean and the conditional mode have the same slopes, and that the estimation error attains the rate $O(\sqrt{s}\lambda_n)$ with no smoothing bias. Simulation studies with up to $p = 1000$ covariates show that the method improves on the Lasso applied to the log-transformed response for Gamma and Weibull responses, and two applications illustrate its use. The method is implemented in the R package ModalNet.
