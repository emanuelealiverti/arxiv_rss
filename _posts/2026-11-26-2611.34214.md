Neighbourhood change is a multidimensional process in which economic, demographic, social and cultural transformations do not necessarily coincide over space and time. However, data driven approaches typically collapse these into single indices or regression models, losing the nuances of individual components. This paper integrates housing market, demographic and social media data (SMD) to characterise different configurations of neighbourhood change in Leeds and Bradford, UK. Rates of change (2014-2019) were estimated over Lower Super Output Areas for house prices relative to the 250 nearest neighbouring areas, and for residential mobility, ethnic composition, unemployment and professional occupation. Approximately 1.6 million geo-tagged tweets, filtered for personal users using a large language model (LLM), were analysed using Structural Topic Models with socio-economic prevalence covariates, LLM based neighbourhood characterisation and text embeddings scored against gentrification and decline archetypes. These measures were used as inputs to a model-based cluster analysis, which identified six distinct neighbourhood types, and a spatially varying multinomial GAM was used to examine the effects of changes in the inputs on cluster membership. The results show repeated divergence between structural and discursive trajectories: rapid turnover that is invisible in local SMD discourse, heritage villages experiencing amenity and incomer pressure, a settled British-Pakistani community whose stability would be mis-characterised as stagnation or decline, and marginal areas accumulating strain with little on-line signal. This suggests that SMD derived measures can provide independent information on neighbourhood character rather than simply confirming structural indicators, although low SMD signals may reflect under-detection rather than an absence of change.
