Hidden Markov models are foundational for sequential inference, yet conditioning on pathwise constraints such as precedence requirements, visitation cardinalities, or monotonic state progression can introduce long-range dependencies that require additional memory for dynamic programming. To deal with this, we present Controller-Augmented Hidden Markov Models (CHMMs), a framework that compiles each constraint into a finite-state controller tracking a sufficient finite history, after which standard forward--backward and Viterbi recursions on the augmented chain compute exact constrained posteriors and maximum a posteriori paths in discrete time and, through uniformization, maximum a posteriori augmented-state sequences at observation times in continuous time. While we establish several theoretical guarantees, a catalog of controller encodings for 11 constraint families operationalizes our framework. Empirically, we evaluate classical and neural decoders with and without controller augmentation on 3 real-world sequence-labeling tasks: gene-structure decoding in \emph{Drosophila melanogaster}, free-living activity recognition in CASAS smart homes, and protocol-defined activity recognition from wearable sensors. Together, theory and experiment characterize when exact controller augmentation is useful and when simpler approaches suffice.
