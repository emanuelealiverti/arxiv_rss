In-play football forecasting models are rarely compared with betting exchange prices, which aggregate information from many market participants. We make this comparison for several goal-arrival models, focusing on a Weibull accelerated failure time model with two extensions: initialising team strength parameters from Betfair Exchange prices at kick-off to capture pre-match market information, and including cumulative shots as a time-varying covariate to capture in-play information. The initialisation, which fits team-strength parameters to 1X2 and over/under prices by squared-error minimisation, applies to any intensity-based goal arrival model. Evaluated across 280 English Premier League matches at minute intervals, the market-initialised model remains measurably weaker than Betfair in-play prices (classification accuracy 68.5% versus 69.5%, ranked probability score 0.1325 versus 0.1281), but retains interpretable team-level parameters and covariate effects. A comparison with a market-implied Poisson model, a Cox process and a Bayesian birth process, all initialised from the same pre-match odds, confirms that market initialisation is the dominant driver of predictive accuracy. A betting simulation against Betfair in-play odds yields a 1.0% return on investment over 37,467 bets (95% interval -3.8% to 5.9%), which we regard as preliminary given the limited sample of matches and prices that may not have been executable.
