summary: Introduces an augmented posterior for deep Gaussian processes to break symmetry

Deep Gaussian processes (DGPs) are flexible priors for compositional regression functions, but inference for intermediate layers faces questions. The basic obstruction is an adjacent-layer symmetry: applying an invertible transformation and its inverse to two adjacent layers leaves the composed regression map unchanged. Under explicit symmetry and prior-comparability conditions, we show that the posterior may not concentrate on any prescribed intermediate-layer representation, even when the composed regression function is estimable. We then introduce an augmented $\rho$-fractional posterior that reweights layer decompositions by a non-saturating identity-deviation penalty. The augmentation changes posterior odds between symmetry-related decompositions by an exponential penalty ratio. Under proper choices of the penalty and a prior mass condition, it preserves function-level fractional posterior contraction, while shrinking the redundant layers towards the identity mapping. Thus, the likelihood does not identify a unique ``true'' set of layers; the augmentation favors identity-biased parsimonious decompositions within an equivalence class of layers; uniqueness of a selected representative is not established.
