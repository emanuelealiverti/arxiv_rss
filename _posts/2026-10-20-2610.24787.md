Cox processes provide a principled Bayesian nonparametric framework for modeling event-based data through an underlying intensity function. However, existing inference methods for estimating the intensity typically rely on computationally expensive Markov chain Monte Carlo sampling or approximate inference techniques. In addition, to ensure tractability, these methods often define priors over indirect latent structures, making them challenging to interpret. In this paper, we introduce the Martingale Posterior Cox Process (MPCP), which recasts Bayesian intensity estimation as inference over unobserved events given the observed event history. This perspective avoids the need to define complex priors while retaining the flexibility of nonparametric modeling. We propose a scalable, GPU-friendly copula-based predictive update, and provide strong theoretical guarantees for the convergence of the predictive resampling algorithm. Empirically, we show that MPCP provides excellent performance in both simulations and real data at a fraction of the computational cost of competing Bayesian approaches.
