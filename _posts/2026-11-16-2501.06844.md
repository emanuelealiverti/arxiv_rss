High-throughput pheno-, geno-, and envirotyping allows characterization of plant genotypes and the trials they are evaluated in, producing different types of data. These different data modalities can be integrated into statistical or machine learning models for genomic prediction in several ways. One common approach within the analysis of multi-environment trial data in plant breeding is to create linear or nonlinear kernels which are subsequently used in linear mixed models for genotype by environment interactions. Current implementations of these kernel-based mixed models present several opportunities in terms of methodological extensions. Here we show how these models can be easily implemented in standard software, allowing direct restricted maximum likelihood estimation of all parameters. We also extend the models by combining the kernels with unstructured covariance matrices for three-way interactions in genotype by environment by management datasets, while simultaneously allowing for environment-specific genetic variances and multiple bandwidth parameters. We show how the models incorporating Gaussian kernels, heterogeneous variances, and management-specific bandwidths maximize the amount of genetic variance captured by environmental covariables, following a biologically intuitive pattern. Furthermore, we show that these models perform best in sparse testing prediction settings using two real-word datasets on wheat and maize. We discuss opportunities regarding models with multiple kernels or kernels obtained after feature selection, as well as the similarities to models regressing phenotypes on latent and observed environmental covariables. Finally, we discuss the flexibility provided by our implementation in terms of modeling complex plant breeding datasets, allowing for straightforward integration of phenomics, enviromics, and genomics.
