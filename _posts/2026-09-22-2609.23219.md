Unobserved confounding is a fundamental challenge in causal inference from observational data. This article develops a mixture-learning perspective, viewing latent confounders as sources of heterogeneity that induce mixture structure in observed data. Under suitable structural and identifiability assumptions, recovering the mixing distribution and component mechanisms enables estimation of interventional distributions and causal estimands. Using variants of Bernoulli mixtures as a running example, we contextualize mixture-learning techniques and their structural assumptions, and connect them to causal inference in panel-data settings, including latent factor models and synthetic interventions.We then consider high-dimensional exponential-family mixtures with dependent outcome trajectories, moving beyond counterfactual means to model counterfactual distributions. We situate this perspective relative to complementary approaches for unobserved confounding. Together, these ideas provide a bridge between mixture learning and causal inference, connecting recent advances in high-dimensional mixture learning to scalable identification and estimation of causal effects while raising new challenges for mixture learning.
