Randomized saturation designs are widely used to study spillover effects in clustered populations. In these designs, clusters are first assigned to treatment saturation levels, and units are then randomized within clusters according to the assigned saturation. This paper develops randomization tests for such experiments under several null hypotheses that arise naturally in spillover analysis. We first compare each unit's untreated potential outcomes at two saturation levels: the partially sharp null requires zero spillover for every unit, while the bounded null imposes a prespecified upper bound on each unit's spillover effect. Both hypotheses can be tested using a common conditional randomization framework that provides finite-sample validity. We next develop a finite-sample valid unconditional randomization test of whether each unit's untreated potential outcome is nondecreasing across saturation levels. Finally, we study weak average-spillover nulls and show that our conditional randomization framework delivers asymptotically valid inference using studentized statistics. Simulations and an application to the Zomba Cash Transfer experiment illustrate the finite-sample behavior and practical implementation of the methods.
