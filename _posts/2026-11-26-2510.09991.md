In networks with feedback, a pairwise instrumental-variable estimate can combine direct and indirect effects. We propose a Bayesian cyclic structural equation model with spike-and-slab priors on direct effects and on error covariances representing unmeasured confounding. Its joint posterior quantifies support for edges, confounding links, and feedback loops. The model has two versions. IV-RGM takes as known the map that lists which responses each instrument affects directly. IV-RGM+ learns this map. A rank condition identifies the model with a known map. For an unknown map, we give conditions on instrument effects and cycle gains that identify the representation with the fewest direct instrument effects. We establish posterior consistency and directed graph recovery under additional conditions. In smaller simulated samples with strong instruments, IV-RGM ranked direct edges more accurately than competing methods when the map was correct. When the supplied map omitted shared targets, IV-RGM+ ranked edges most accurately and reduced false discoveries relative to fixing the incorrect map. Weak instruments limited recovery across methods. In B cells, the analysis with a known map gave limited support to feedback among inhibitory receptor genes. Both versions are available in an R package on CRAN (https://cran.r-project.org/package=MR.RGM).
