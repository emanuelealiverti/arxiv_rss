We describe a framework for building low-rank separable expansions to model spectroscopic data from systems with time-varying spectra, in settings where the spectral dimension is finely sampled (in wavelength), but the temporal dimension is sparsely and possibly irregulary sampled. The motivating application is detection and characterization of small exoplanets, e.g., Earth-like planets around Sun-like stars. Such planets cannot be seen directly, but can be detected via the minute Doppler shifts of the host star spectrum due to the star's motion as the planet and star revolve around their mutual center of mass. Current instruments are reaching the precision necessary to detect the ~10 cm/s motions corresponding to an exo-Earth. But the small Doppler shifts from such low-amplitude orbital motion can be masked or mimicked by the effects of stellar activity on the surface of the host star. e.g., starspots (dark regions), faculae (bright filaments), etc. One technique astronomers have used to try to model the stellar activity component of the signal is PCA, applied to an ensemble of spectra collected over time. But PCA neglects the continuous function nature of a spectrum at a given time, and the continuous evolution of spectra over time. We address model building from a function approximation perspective, via the asymmetric Hilbert-Schmidt decomposition (HSD). This provides a PCA-like low-rank decomposition of a dynamic spectrum, but it explicitly accounts for time dependence, both estimating temporal basis functions, and accounting for irregular temporal sampling when estimating spectral basis functions. We show that HSD improves the fidelity of a fixed-order model of the dynamic spectrum of a Sun-like star with a starspot, with mean squared error for spectral basis functions many times smaller than achieved via PCA when spectra are randomly sampled sparsely and irregularly in time.
