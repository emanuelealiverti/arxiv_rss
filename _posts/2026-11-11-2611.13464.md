Industrial sensor measurements often form ordered functional profiles in which persistent operating-regime changes may be confined to short within-cycle regions. Fixed projections can dilute such changes, whereas data-adaptive representation learning complicates reliable statistical testing. This paper proposes adaptive functional change-point detection (AdaFCP) for retrospective detection of a single functional mean change. A compact window and neural basis learn a change-sensitive functional projection by matching the CUSUM path of projected scores to single-change templates. Standardized peak-CUSUM scanning then provides the detection statistic and transition estimate, while the fitted weights identify the contributing within-cycle region. Theoretical analysis characterizes the population path-matching signal and derives an optimal-direction signal-to-noise benchmark. Full-pipeline permutation calibration accounts for adaptive representation learning and restart selection, providing finite-sample false-positive control under exchangeability. A decision-preserving early-stopping variant, AdaFCP-ES, further reduces calibration effort without altering the fixed-budget test decision. Simulations demonstrate advantages over several fixed representations for localized oscillatory changes, while strong analytic and multiscale controls remain competitive. Applications to injection-molding pressure profiles and a nuisance-matched hydraulic benchmark illustrate transition localization and interpretable within-cycle diagnostics, while highlighting the need for dependence-aware calibration in naturally ordered industrial data.
