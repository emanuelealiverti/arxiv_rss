summary: Introduces M-function predictive distributions for interval-censored data,

Nonparametric predictive inference specifies the distribution of a future observation through M-functions, which are basic probability assignments on intervals. Here such a specification is proposed for data censored on the left, on the right or within intervals of arbitrary width. It is the union of two M-functions. The first rests on a post-data assumption under which each censored observation divides its predictive mass equally amongst the observations with regions inside its own, and it reproduces the right-censored theory exactly. The second combines the Turnbull estimator with one unconstrained unit of predictive mass. The resulting survival bounds contain the Turnbull estimator, lie inside the bounds from all completions of the data and need no enumeration. In simulated inspection schemes they were calibrated for fixed events and for the rank of a further observation, while the second member alone was not. Equal weights on all orderings allowed by the data give the first member whenever the regions are nested or disjoint. The method is compared with the Turnbull estimator on two interval-censored studies.
