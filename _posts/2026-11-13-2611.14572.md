The standard rule in unconstrained causal policy learning is simple: treat individuals with nonnegative CATEs. But this rule can fail when treatment eligibility is strategically manipulable. After a policy is announced, individuals may change the covariates used for assignment without changing their underlying treatment effects. We study how such responses affect treatment allocation and welfare. Using an access-burden representation, we characterize strategic treatment and decompose welfare loss into missed beneficial treatment and harmful treatment. For linear CATEs and quadratic manipulation costs, we derive closed-form threshold corrections. When manipulation costs are homogeneous, a single correction recovers the ideal positive-CATE allocation. With heterogeneous costs, welfare maximization, pointwise safety, and minimax group welfare regret can favor different thresholds. Simulations illustrate these tradeoffs and examine the effects of estimating treatment effects and behavioral responses.
