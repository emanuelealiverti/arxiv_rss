The integration of external information can substantially improve the efficiency of statistical inference in an internal study, but is often complicated by between-study heterogeneity. Additional challenges arise when external studies examine auxiliary outcomes rather than on the target outcomes and provide only summary statistics. To address these challenges, we propose a semiparametric framework that incorporates external summary statistics into the internal analysis via the generalized method of moments. To accommodate between-study heterogeneity, the external summaries are formulated within a random-effects model centered on the internal parameter estimates and subsequently embedded into the internal estimating equations. We establish the asymptotic properties of the proposed estimator. While uncertainty from the first-stage estimation vanishes as the number of external studies increases, it remains non-negligible where the number of external studies is moderate. In this setting, we show that a hybrid bootstrap procedure correctly propagates this uncertainty and consistently estimates the asymptotic covariance of the proposed estimator, thereby ensuring valid inference. We further derive theoretical efficiency bounds and demonstrate robust finite-sample performance through simulation studies. In an application to colorectal cancer tumor sequencing data, we show that integrating external information of auxiliary outcomes reveals molecular subtype-specific etiologic associations that would not be detected using the internal data alone.
