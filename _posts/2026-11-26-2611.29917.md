Tensor regression is challenged by the exponential dimensionality of tensor-valued covariates and their complex multi-mode dependence. Existing methods therefore often rely on simple models, such as linear tensor regression and tensor additive models, which are computationally tractable but impose restrictive structural assumptions and may limit predictive accuracy. To address these limitations, we propose a tensor neural network that learns low-dimensional, interpretable, and prediction-sufficient representations while preserving the multi-mode structure of tensor data. The model captures nonlinear effects for accurate response prediction while retaining the interpretability of classical linear tensor regression. Theoretically, we establish selection consistency for the minimal representation dimension $K^*$ necessary for prediction and show that the estimator attains a representation-adaptive non-asymptotic error rate. Specifically, the rate improves from the ambient-dimensional order $n^{-\frac{\beta}{\mathcal{D}+2\beta}}$ to $n^{-\frac{\beta}{K^*+2\beta}}$, where $\mathcal{D}=\prod_{j=1}^M P_j$ is the ambient dimension. This demonstrates that recovering the low-dimensional representation is crucial for mitigating the curse of dimensionality in tensor regression. Moreover, minimizing the derived generalization error bound yields explicit tuning-parameter choices for computationally lightweight tuning. Extensive simulations and real-data analyses show consistent performance across settings of varying complexity. Unlike black-box deep learning models, the framework supports direct visualization and interpretation of learned tensor representations without external post hoc tools.
