In decision-making under uncertainty, additional information can improve decisions but is often costly to acquire. The Value of Information (VoI) quantifies the expected benefit of different information-gathering strategies. The main computational burden in VoI analysis arises from evaluating the expected utility of a strategy over possible outcomes. As each outcome entails solving a computationally expensive optimization problem under uncertainty to determine the optimal decision, efficient estimation of this expectation is crucial. This work focuses on sampling-based estimation and investigates three classical variance-reduction techniques, namely control variates, stratified sampling, and importance sampling, as well as combinations thereof. In particular, we establish a connection between two common VoI formulations through a control variate framework and show how this relationship can be exploited to obtain more efficient VoI estimators. The methodology is assessed using three test cases. The first is an analytical example with a known reference solution, the second involves a fatigue reliability and the third a slope stability application. Our results demonstrate that the relative performance of the sampling methods depends strongly on the problem characteristics. However, suitable combinations of variance-reduction techniques consistently improve efficiency and provide stable VoI estimates across a range of scenarios.
