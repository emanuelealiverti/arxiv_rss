Many complex networks involve time-varying relations between more than two nodes. Such networks are naturally modeled by temporal hypergraphs. We propose the Hawkes Hypergraph Copy Model (HHCM), a semi-mechanistic model for temporal hypergraphs that combines a Hawkes process to model the timestamps of hyperedges with a noisy copy mechanism to model the nodes present in hyperedges. Each hyperedge is either generated as an exogenous event with a baseline rate or an endogenous event as a noisy copy of a previous hyperedge. Our proposed model can be used for credit attribution, where the objective is to quantify the influence or contribution of a prior hyperedge to a current one. We apply the HHCM to perform attribution on several real network datasets with higher-order relations, including email networks and militarized interstate dispute networks.
