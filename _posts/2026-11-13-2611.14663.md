Characterizing the relationship between brain anatomical wiring and functional coordination remains a fundamental challenge in computational neuroscience. This difficulty primarily arises because structure-function coupling in the human brain is spatially heterogeneous, often localized to subnetworks, and inherently difficult to align across disparate representational modalities. We introduce Orthogonal Sparse Subgraph Alignment (OSSA), a principled and interpretable framework designed to identify compact structural cliques that exhibit maximal concordance with functional connectivity (FC) across subjects. Within each FC-derived community, OSSA optimizes an alignment objective over an orthogonal transformation, which absorbs coordinate mismatch between the two connectivity modalities, and a sparse node-selection vector, which identifies the induced structural connectivity (SC) subgraph. Furthermore, for canonical OSSA within a fixed population community, we establish local statistical guarantees under the required assumptions. With high probability, an empirical local maximizer exists in a specified neighborhood of the population target, and every local maximizer in that neighborhood exactly recovers the active support and satisfies a nonasymptotic error bound for the aligned functional projector. Extensive empirical evaluations on simulated data, alongside analyses of the Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development (ABCD) baseline and longitudinal cohorts, demonstrate that OSSA successfully recovers aligned structural cliques under latent cross-modal rotations. Ultimately, the proposed methodology significantly improves out-of-sample SC-FC alignment over size-matched baselines and identifies compact structural supports with longitudinal consistency in node selection, while multi-support diagnostics suggest a dominant aligned core within the estimated FC communities.
