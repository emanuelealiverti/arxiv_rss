Identification of assignment effects among compliers often relies on the untestable assumption of principal ignorability. Sensitivity analyses based only on outcome means and ranges can admit complier distributions incompatible with the observed data. We develop a distributional sensitivity model preserving the full observed outcome distributions and identified complier proportions. The challenge is to recover latent complier distributions within observed mixtures while ensuring compatibility across assignment arms. We characterize exactly which pairs arise from a single compatible full-data law, obtaining sharp bounds on effects and policy values. This characterization shows how complier proportions constrain departures from principal ignorability and why informative bounds remain without a finite sensitivity restriction. Accounting for principal scores in both population weights and sensitivity limits, we establish conditions for doubly robust consistency, semiparametric efficiency, and conservative bounds under quantile misspecification. Double robustness requires consistent principal score estimation. We also develop maximin decision-tree policies with value consistency and inference guarantees. Numerical studies demonstrate gains from retaining distributional information, and an Oregon Health Insurance Experiment application examines implications for complier effects and assignment policies.
