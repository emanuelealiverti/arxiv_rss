We introduce a family of smooth $q$-polynomial losses for robust joint regression--scale estimation. The family contains the Cauchy loss at $q=1$ and converges pointwise to the squared loss as $q$ increases. For every finite $q$, its regression score is bounded and redescending, while its scale score is bounded and permits explicit analysis of scale existence. We formulate simultaneous estimation through a surrogate likelihood with a common scale calibration $\kappa=1/2$ and select $q$ by maximizing the normalized surrogate likelihood over a finite candidate set. We investigate existence, large-sample behaviour, and the robustness implications of the loss family and discuss how optional modification of the scale score, for example by taking $\kappa=q/2$, can increase the threshold against scale explosion. This breakdown-oriented adjustment is distinct from the baseline estimator and requires its own joint breakdown analysis. An MM-type algorithm provides a practical implementation. Numerical investigations assess the resulting robustness--efficiency trade-off under different tail regimes.
