AI is dramatically changing the statistical research landscape. I argue that production of a distinct category of statistical knowledge, which I refer to as explicational statistics, is actually becoming more valuable as AI increasingly automates other parts of the research process. I define explicational statistics, contextualize it in terms of other forms of statistical knowledge, and explain why I think the rise of AI makes it more valuable. I demonstrate my framework by first developing a new dimension reduction method, Ambient Embedding Projection, that can simultaneously visualize the intrinsic geometry of a data manifold along with how that manifold is embedded into ambient space. I then use my new method to conceive of a new statistical property of a collection of manifolds, related to metric distortion, that in single-cell biology can capture the ensemble behavior of cell populations. I apply these ideas to single-nuclei RNA-seq data from the brains of mice from different behavioral conditions to reveal potential cellular mechanisms of chronic social defeat stress.
