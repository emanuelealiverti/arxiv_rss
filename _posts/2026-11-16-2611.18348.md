Understanding how dependence among component lifetimes affects the reliability of redundant systems is a central problem in reliability engineering. In this paper, we develop a framework for quantifying the impact of multivariate dependence on $k$-out-of-$n$ system reliability relative to the independent benchmark. Using an orthogonal Legendre expansion of the copula density, we derive an exact decomposition that separates the dependence structure from the system architecture. This representation identifies how individual dependence contributions are transmitted to system reliability through explicit structural weights. An application to multivariate Farlie-Gumbel-Morgenstern copulas demonstrates that higher-order dependence can produce distinct reliability effects even when all bivariate copulas remain unchanged. Our findings provide a systematic approach to quantifying and interpreting the effects of multivariate dependence on the reliability of redundant systems.
