Epidemiological analyses often suffer from covariate data quality issues: measurement error in continuous predictors and misclassification of categorical variables. Existing logistic regression correction methods mostly handle only one error type or a single misclassified variable, with limited tools for multiple misclassified categorical exposures together with correlated measurement error in continuous covariates. To address this gap, we extend regression calibration for logistic regression to jointly accommodate multiple continuous covariates with correlated additive measurement error and two or more misclassified categorical predictors. We build a unified framework integrating additive measurement error models and misclassification transition matrices, deriving the regression calibration estimator (RCE) using Kronecker product algebra. This computationally feasible approach extends naturally to more than two misclassified categorical variables. We evaluate RCE via simulations across varied misclassification rates, error magnitudes, sample sizes and effect sizes, comparing it with the naive estimator that ignores measurement error. We apply RCE to the EPIC-InterAct male case-cohort data to adjust for correlated error in protein and energy intake, plus misclassification in binary physical activity and three-category alcohol consumption. Results show that neglecting these error sources produces substantial bias and misleading inference for main and interaction effects in nutritional epidemiology. While the RCE is a practical correction tool, it treats validation nuisance parameters as known and does not account for their estimation uncertainty.
