A subgroup analysis searching a trial's covariates or many prespecified subgroups often singles out a subgroup for its extremity, interpreted as if prespecified. Selection inflates the estimate; for a given selection rule, a correction removes the inflation. Two questions belong to the rule: which candidate to return, and whether to declare none. A third is whether inference extends to the patients left outside. We treat all three for interpretable subgroups, defined by conjunctions of covariate cuts, generated by forest search, a causal forest or a cross-fit surface estimator under one set of rules, each a modelling choice trading size against effect. Forest search screens candidates on splitting consistency, the rate at which effects replicate across half-splits; the screen is complementary-pairs stability selection in closed form, and the law of the maximum over the family gives the family-wise false-declaration rate when every candidate's effect is at least a clinically specified benefit. Refit-free multiplier resampling gives adjusted one-sided bounds (subgroup and complement), joined in a two-subgroup claim, with coverage asymptotically exact under separated selection, characterized under local competition, and conditional on a family proposed from the outcomes. Two trial applications, and simulations built from them, cover survival, binary and continuous endpoints.
