We study regularized estimation in high-dimensional vector autoregressions under physical dependence and row-wise approximate sparsity using the Broken Adaptive Ridge (BAR) estimator. BAR is an iteratively reweighted Ridge procedure whose fixed-point first-order conditions imply a coordinatewise thresholding rule that produces exact zeros. We show that the BAR estimation error can be analyzed on the union of the population support above the BAR threshold and the support selected in the sample. The size of this union can be controlled at the rate implied by approximate sparsity, allowing us to establish the required restricted-curvature condition and derive L1, L2, and prediction error rates when the number of regressors grows polynomially with the sample size and may exceed it. Under exact sparsity, the resulting rates recover the familiar rates for sparse high-dimensional estimation. Monte Carlo experiments across a range of high-dimensional VAR designs show that BAR delivers strong estimation and forecasting performance, and retains a high share of relevant coefficients relative to Lasso, adaptive Lasso, and SCAD. An application to U.S. inflation forecasting using FRED-MD shows particularly strong performance at the one-month horizon.
