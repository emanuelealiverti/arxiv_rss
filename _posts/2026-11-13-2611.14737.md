Nonparametric regression conventionally assumes equispaced observations, a premise often violated by empirical data with irregular sampling and long-memory noise. This study establishes the asymptotic mean square ($L^2$) consistency of a weighted Nadaraya-Watson (wN-W) estimator for stochastic processes sampled at random times and perturbed by fractional Brownian motion (fBm). Leveraging the occupation time formula and Fernique's Theorem, we derive exact parametric restrictions on bandwidth and sampling rates ensuring convergence. We validate these theoretical findings via extensive simulations evaluating the mean squared error under varying noise persistence. Crucially, we explicitly benchmark the performance of the estimator under random sampling times against classical deterministic grids, while assessing the relative efficiency of quartic versus Gaussian kernels. Finally, we introduce an interactive Shiny application to dynamically visualize the admissible parameter space, and we empirically validate our theoretical framework by applying the estimator to the Vostok ice core paleoclimatological dataset. This real-world application demonstrates the practical capacity of the methodology to extract macroscopic trends while robustly avoiding severe overfitting under random sampling times.\
