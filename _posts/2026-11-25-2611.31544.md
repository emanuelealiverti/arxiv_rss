Runway incursions are one of ICAO's five high-risk occurrence categories, yet airport-level comparisons of incursion risk in the United States still rest on raw counts or on fixed-count thresholds that select on sampling noise. We assemble an airport-fiscal-year panel of 565 FAA-towered airports holding 27,079 runway-incursion records from the FAA Runway Safety database as held in the FlightFinder corpus (himaxym.com), normalized by tower operations from the FAA Terminal Area Forecast (ATADS/OPSNET actuals). A hierarchical negative-binomial model with log operations as an exposure offset, airport random effects, fiscal-year effects and traffic-mix covariates yields, for every airport, a shrinkage-adjusted rate per million operations, an exceedance probability relative to the national median, and a posterior rank with a 90% interval in the manner of Goldstein and Spiegelhalter. Severity (Category A-D) is modeled separately as an ordinal outcome, and empirical-Bayes and fully Bayesian shrinkage are compared. The crude national rate is 29.4 incursions per million operations (95% interval 29.1-29.8), with an index of dispersion of 8.61. Between-airport dispersion after exposure adjustment is sigma_u = 1.05: an airport one standard deviation above the mean carries 2.84 times the national rate. 199 of 565 airports exceed the national median with probability above 0.9, and the 90% rank interval at the median airport spans 134 positions, so much of the league table is statistically indistinguishable from its middle. Severity and frequency respond to different determinants: the airport-level rank correlation between them is -0.08 (90% interval -0.14 to -0.02), so a frequency ranking carries little information about where a severe outcome is likely. The results offer a reproducible, uncertainty-aware alternative to count thresholds for prioritizing runway-safety interventions.
