Asymmetric multidimensional scaling (AMDS) methods represent asymmetric dissimilarity data in a low-dimensional space. Existing approaches include the highly flexible unfolding model and the parsimonious slide-vector model. However, a highly flexible model is not necessarily optimal for adequately representing asymmetric relationships, and a model with an intermediate level of complexity may be more effective. In this study, we propose a new AMDS model that integrates the unfolding and slide-vector models by incorporating a clustered Lasso penalty. By fusing similar slide vectors, the proposed method can represent intermediate model structures and adjust model complexity according to the observed data. In numerical simulations, we compared the proposed method with existing methods by varying the number of groups, the number of objects, and the number of dimensions. The results demonstrated that the proposed method achieved high estimation accuracy across various asymmetric structures. Furthermore, in an application to cola brand-switching data, the proposed method achieved the highest accuracy in reproducing asymmetric relationships among the three methods compared and yielded a lower stress value than the slide-vector model. These findings suggest that adaptively adjusting model complexity may be effective for representing asymmetric relationships more accurately.
