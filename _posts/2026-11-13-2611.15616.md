We propose an identification theory for stochastic interventions on the transition dynamics in continuous-time multi-state models. Interventions are formalized as local modifications of a likelihood satisfying a causal invariance property. We show that identification from observed data is achieved when no unmeasured process simultaneously affects an intervened local characteristic and a competing or subsequent non-intervened local characteristic, covariate local characteristic, or censoring intensity. Our framework allows for intervening on both the timing and type of events, accommodates measured and latent covariates as time-varying marked point processes, and extends to right-censored data. We exemplify by considering identifiability in two concrete applications: one studying the effect of changing the timing of intrauterine insemination on the probability of pregnancy, and one studying the effect of changing the type of dysphagia relieving surgery on the risk of death in esophageal cancer patients.
