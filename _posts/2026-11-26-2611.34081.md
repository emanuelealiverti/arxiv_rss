Parametric frontier restrictions and unobserved time heterogeneity can distort efficiency measurement in panel data. This paper develops a Bayesian stochastic frontier model with a Gaussian process prior and unrestricted individual and time effects that may be correlated with inputs. Conditional on inefficiency, orthogonal projection eliminates both sets of fixed effects, yielding a restricted likelihood that retains observation-specific inefficiency. Monte Carlo experiments show competitive performance under correct parametric specification and improved performance when benchmark models impose an incorrect frontier functional form or omit time effects. An application to Spanish dairy farms observed from 1999 to 2010 yields similar average technical efficiency across specifications, but a lower posterior mean of average returns to scale, with greater uncertainty, under the Gaussian process frontier. Models with year effects also yield more stable estimated efficiency profiles than the benchmark with a quadratic trend and time-input interactions. These findings show that conclusions about scale economies and efficiency dynamics depend on both frontier flexibility and the treatment of time heterogeneity.
