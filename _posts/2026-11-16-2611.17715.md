Canonical correlation analysis (CCA) is a widely used statistical tool for exploring the dependence structure between two sets of variables. An important issue in CCA is determining the rank. Among information-based criteria for rank selection, the Akaike information criterion (AIC) and the Bayesian information criterion (BIC) are two commonly used approaches. Both rely on the number of free parameters to quantify model complexity, which may not adequately account for the underlying dependence structure when the assumed model is misspecified. As a result, AIC and BIC may exhibit unsatisfactory performance, particularly when the remaining canonical correlations do not follow the assumed low-dimensional structure. To alleviate this difficulty, we adopt the idea of the generalized information criterion (GIC) to develop a data-adaptive measure of model complexity for CCA rank selection. The proposed complexity measure incorporates the magnitudes of the sample canonical correlations, thereby accounting for the spectral structure of the data and improving robustness to model misspecification. We investigate the asymptotic properties of the proposed GIC under a high-dimensional setting in which the sample size and both variable dimensions grow proportionally. Our theoretical analysis aims to characterize the conditions under which GIC can distinguish signal canonical correlations from noise and achieve more reliable rank selection than conventional information criteria.
