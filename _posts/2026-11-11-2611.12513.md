In archaeology and geochronology, stratigraphic information constrains the relative chronological order of dated samples. Bayesian chronological models incorporate these constraints either through the prior, or by projecting an unconstrained posterior distribution onto the set of ordered chronologies. Dating measurements may nevertheless appear inconsistent with their stratigraphic position, owing to disturbances that do not justify discarding them. We introduce different robust projection maps based on the Huber loss and characterize the non-uniqueness of the considered maps which justifies a Tikhonov regularization. The robust maps also decompose each sample into a monotone baseline and a spike, which flags questionable samples. We adapt the Sequential Block Merging algorithm for the Huber-type losses, and propose a data-driven heuristic for selecting the hyperparameter on which each loss depends on. Simulation studies and geochronological case studies illustrate the gains in robustness and their limits.
