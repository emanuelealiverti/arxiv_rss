This study performs a beta regression analysis to investigate how country-level cognitive skills---measured using harmonized large-scale international educational assessments and expressed on an IQ-type scale---are associated with the prevalence of atheism in 131 countries. We find that this association is nonlinear: at low levels of cognitive skills, increases are associated with lower expected levels of atheism, whereas beyond a threshold, they are associated with higher expected atheism rates. This turning point is estimated to occur at approximately 65 on the IQ-type scale. The strength and location of the relationship vary across regions. We estimate elasticities of expected atheism rates with respect to cognitive skills for all countries in the sample and introduce the concept of the atheism gap to identify countries where observed prevalence is substantially higher or lower than predicted by their conditioning variables.
