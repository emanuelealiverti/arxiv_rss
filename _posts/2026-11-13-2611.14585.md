Sleep is scored afterwards in 30-second epochs as wake (W), the transitional stage N1, or N2. We study EEG forecasts, issued every 30 s from the preceding 30 s of EEG alone, of whether the first scored N2 epoch will begin within five minutes, on Sleep-EDF. A Brier score pooled over decisions made before and after the input is scored N1 does not show when the gain arose. In our own prespecified one-shot held-out comparisons, the spectral model SpecLR-18 improved on a training prevalence constant by 18.3% in the internal test, whereas its prespecified external result was inconclusive. Joining the retrospectively scored stage of each input epoch to the same frozen predictions, almost all of that internal-test Brier difference was attributed to N1-scored decisions; a diagnostic reference told only the scored stage reached 26.3% in the internal test and 29.5% in the external cohort, above the spectral forecasts in point estimate though not every difference excluded zero; and within-stage EEG advantages seen in single cohorts were not consistent across cohorts, with no consistent wake-stratum improvement. These references use labels unavailable online and cannot reveal what a model represents; they show why pooled forecasting scores need state-conditioned interpretation before being read as early anticipation.
