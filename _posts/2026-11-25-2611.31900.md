Many causal estimands depend on the joint distribution of potential outcomes, which is generally not identifiable because only one potential outcome can be observed for each unit. To obtain identification, researchers often impose assumptions such as monotonicity and exclusion restriction that constrain the support of this joint distribution. For treatments and outcomes that each take finitely many values, we define a similarity matrix whose entries count, for each pair of potential outcome strata, the number of treatments yielding the same outcome. We then prove that, under unconfoundedness, identification holds if and only if this matrix is invertible, yielding a criterion directly verifiable from the support. In addition, we derive an identification formula for the joint distribution under these support restrictions and show that an average causal estimand is identifiable if and only if its integrand admits an additive representation on the restricted support. Finally, we derive sharp testable implications of the support restriction. Our results unify and generalize a broad range of identification results in the literature.
