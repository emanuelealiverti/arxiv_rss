Principal component analysis (PCA) optimizes aggregate reconstruction accuracy but may represent heterogeneous groups unevenly. We study fairness-regularized PCA, which balances aggregate reconstruction risk against worst-group reconstruction regret, and establish its population and statistical properties. First, we characterize the reconstruction-fairness trade-off and show that, under a nondegeneracy condition, a small fairness penalty reduces worst-group regret to first order while increasing aggregate reconstruction risk only to second order. This reveals a vanishing initial marginal cost of improving fairness. We also identify conditions under which the regularization path remains locally constant. A minimax spectral characterization shows that, under an optimal effective eigengap condition, the convex relaxation is exact and Fair PCA is equivalent to ordinary PCA with endogenously adjusted group weights. Second, we develop a statistical theory for estimating fairness-regularized principal subspaces. A uniform eigengap over the set of possibly nonunique dual optimizers implies polyhedral identification and quadratic growth, yielding first-order perturbation bounds and convergence rates that accommodate growing dimensions, ranks, and numbers of groups. Third, we establish uniform recovery of the reconstruction-fairness path and theoretical guarantees for data-adaptive penalty selection. Simulations and an NHANES accelerometry application demonstrate substantial improvements in worst-group representation quality with minimal loss of aggregate reconstruction accuracy.
