Stochastic Galerkin schemes (SGS) are used to approximate solutions to differential equations with stochastic parameters. In particular, they express the solution with an infinite orthogonal functional basis of the parameter space and derive a deterministic surrogate system of PDEs whose solution approximates that of the original stochastic problem. When the parameters are correlated, the infinite series representation of the solution does not provably converge for all joint distributions; further, classic decorrelating transformations (e.g., Rosenblatt, Nataf) often introduce known numerical instabilities. Rahman (2017) established convergence in probability and distribution for correlated Gaussian parameters when the basis functionals are multivariate Hermite polynomials (MHPs), where these polynomials are only weakly orthogonal with respect to the underlying joint density. Due to weak orthogonality, the $N$th moments of MHPs that emerge during Galerkin projection do not trivially decompose into products of one-dimensional expectations via tensorization. Namely, an $N$th-order moment has no closed-form expression for $N > 2$ and consequently is estimated numerically. Because the number of such moments grows combinatorially with parameter dimension and polynomial degree, approximating each one individually via sampling or quadrature can quickly become cost-prohibitive. In this paper, we introduce and prove several foundational properties of MHPs. These results culminate in an explicit formula for evaluating the $N = 3$ moments (triple products) directly without numerical estimation.
