Watermarking enables detection of text generated by large language models, but practical review also requires quantifying retained signals within content of interest. Readers may choose passages based on their content and refine their selections after examining the watermark evidence. We develop simultaneous lower confidence bounds on the fraction of selected tokens that retain a watermark signal. The bounds hold jointly for the full document and any passages selected during review. We develop structure-adaptive closed testing (SACT), which uses a working model of sequential signal clustering to construct scores for calibrated sign-based tests. Under a conditional null model, the bounds have finite-sample simultaneous validity even when the working model is misspecified. An exact reference-family representation recovers the full closed-testing bounds while enabling efficient repeated queries. In a constructed database-recovery review, SACT detects document-wide watermark signals and provides lower confidence bounds on watermark proportions in selected passages. Assessing related passages together gives a sharper lower bound on their combined signal count. In simulations spanning two watermarking mechanisms and a range of signal strengths and densities, SACT generally has higher power than competing methods.
