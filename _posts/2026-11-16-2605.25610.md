Classification of matches played in the last rounds of sports competitions is a well-established tool for evaluating tournament designs. Our paper offers the first comparison of deterministic and probabilistic match classification models by analysing the most prominent example of four-team round-robin competitions, the group stage of the FIFA World Cup. We show that both models are highly relevant in practice: all (four) deterministic and (six) probabilistic match types occurred in the 2014 and 2018 FIFA World Cups, respectively. The probabilistic model, which accounts for the relative benefits of attacking and defending, provides a deeper insight; for instance, the competitive matches from the deterministic approach can be of any of the six probabilistic types. Therefore, the probabilistic model is used to decompose the impact of three reforms introduced in the 2026 FIFA World Cup: the expansion to 48 teams, the modified qualification rules, and head-to-head tie-breakers.
