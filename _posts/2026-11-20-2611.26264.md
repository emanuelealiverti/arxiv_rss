Training-data auditing asks whether data of interest were used to train a large language model (LLM), a question central to privacy auditing, copyright protection, and benchmark-contamination detection. These concerns are particularly relevant to the use of copyrighted books and scientific papers in pre-training, as well as synthetic text and medical question-answer records in fine-tuning. In practice, training membership signals may be concentrated in only a few passages of a long document, making it important to aggregate evidence within each document without diluting these localized signals. Beyond individual documents, auditing a target collection requires an overall assessment based on the document-level evidence. In this paper, we propose a unified statistical framework for document-level membership inference, target-collection global auditing, and suspicious-subset identification. We integrate complementary chunk-level membership evidence with Cauchy combination to construct document scores for document-level membership inference. The document scores and their calibrated p-values further enable an overall assessment of training-data collection use and the identification of suspicious documents through multiple testing. Under the stated exchangeability conditions, we establish finite-sample validity of document-level p-values, type-I error control for global auditing, and false discovery rate control for subset identification. Case studies on BookTection, Pile-arXiv, Cosmopedia, and HealthCareMagic demonstrate the effectiveness and reliability of the proposed framework in auditing pre-training and fine-tuning data.
