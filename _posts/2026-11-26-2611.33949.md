The next German Census (2031) is planned to be the country's first fully register-based census, relying exclusively on administrative data sources. In addition to the population registers, ten further administrative registers are envisaged, covering a wide range of information, from driving licences to agricultural social insurance. This study simulates potential data-quality challenges in linking these registers using quasi-identifiers (QIDs), such as names and dates of birth. Using a synthetic population dataset (N=80,206,770), we systematically vary experimental factors related to register characteristics. Across the 1,458 experimental conditions studied, results vary considerably. Some plausible scenarios range from 500,000 to 1,000,000 false-negative linkage errors per linked register. These findings suggest that implementing an automated register-based census using the currently envisaged registers may prove challenging and could require substantial manual post-processing to achieve acceptable data quality.
