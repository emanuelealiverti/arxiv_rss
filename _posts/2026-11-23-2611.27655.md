Firms routinely conduct randomized experiments, creating archives that could improve treatment-effect estimation in new experiments. However, past experiments evaluate related but different interventions, so their estimates may be biased for the current target. We study when and how borrowing from multiple auxiliary experiments can reduce the mean squared error (MSE) of a single treatment-effect estimate. In the oracle case, when biases and variances are known, borrowing from even one biased experiment strictly reduces MSE. In practice, we propose three shrinkage estimators and characterize their asymptotic MSE as the number of auxiliary experiments grows. Our analysis shows that average auxiliary bias and cross-study bias dispersion affect the relative performance of these estimators. As expected, smaller average bias magnitude generally improves performance. Surprisingly, bias dispersion can both benefit and harm our first estimator, the general plug-in estimator. On one hand, a more dispersed bias allows flexibility in cancelling the bias. On the other hand, the bias cancellation is imperfect as we only have an estimate of the bias, leading to overfitting. Motivated by this tradeoff, we propose a pooled plug-in estimator that restricts study-specific reweighting to limit this overfitting. However, both plug-in estimators can perform poorly when average bias is large. To address this, we develop a fixed-weight estimator with a hypothesis-testing gate that borrows only when the aggregate auxiliary mean is not significantly different from the target mean. Simulations illustrate the relative strengths of these estimators across bias environments and the relevance of the limiting analysis for finite collections of experiments. We also provide managerial guidance on which shrinkage estimator to use based on average bias and bias dispersion.
