In many applications, variability itself carries important predictive information beyond the mean, and its association with an outcome may further depend on the underlying mean trajectory. However, existing functional regression approaches primarily rely on mean-based representations of functional predictors, while variability is often ignored or reduced to scalar summaries. We propose a functional regression framework that incorporates latent mean and variance processes as predictors, together with their mean-variance interaction, allowing the predictive contribution of variability to depend on the underlying mean trajectory. We establish consistency and convergence rates for the estimated coefficient functions and interaction surface, accounting for reconstruction errors, increasing observation frequency, and diverging truncation dimensions without requiring independence between the latent processes. Applications to real data demonstrate substantial gains in predictive accuracy from incorporating the variance process, with the proposed approach outperforming competing functional regression methods in the Tecator application and revealing localized mean-variance interaction patterns.
