Accurate characterization of intra-urban temperature variability is essential for heat-health surveillance, yet official monitoring networks are too sparse to resolve fine-scale gradients, particularly in Global South cities. We present BRUCE, a Bayesian hierarchical spatiotemporal model, fitted with the Integrated Nested Laplace Approximation (INLA) and a Stochastic Partial Differential Equation (SPDE) spatial component, that fuses four heterogeneous temperature sources for Rio de Janeiro, Brazil: official weather stations, low-cost IoT sensors, citizen-science personal weather stations and ERA5-Land reanalysis. The model accounts for class- and station-specific measurement bias and estimates a continuous spatial field that evolves day to day through an autoregressive (AR1) structure. Fitted independently to nine monthly windows (January, June, November; 2023--2025), the elevation coefficient was significant in all windows and of consistent magnitude in eight ($-$1.08 to $-$1.38 \textdegree{}C per SD). In leave-region-out cross-validation against LCZ4r, accuracy was comparable (RMSE differences within station-level bootstrap uncertainty in all three windows), while BRUCE produced predictions at every held-out station, including those where LCZ4r is masked, and preserved finer-scale spatial structure than LCZ4r's discrete zone classification. Applied to 241 primary care territories, BRUCE's posterior exceedance probabilities allowed 96 to be classified with high confidence (model-based classifications), whereas a constant-width interval derived from LCZ4r cross-validation errors supported confident classification of none. The framework offers a statistically principled, operationally viable basis for intra-urban heat monitoring in data-heterogeneous settings.
