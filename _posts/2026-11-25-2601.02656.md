Clustering is a central tool in biomedical research for discovering heterogeneous patient subpopulations, where group boundaries are often diffuse rather than sharply separated. While soft clustering methods such as fuzzy $c$-means (FCM) allow mixed memberships, principled statistical inference for fuzzy clustering remains limited. We propose a weighted fuzzy $c$-means (WFCM) framework for settings with cluster-size imbalance. Cluster-specific weights $\mathbf{w}$ re-balance the classic FCM criterion to adjust the relative influence of clusters of unequal size, and we construct a normalized working density from the weighted objective with scale parameter $\sigma$ and fuzziness parameter $m$. Estimation is performed via FCM-style alternating initialization followed by numerical likelihood refinement; the intractable normalizing constant is approximated by importance sampling using a data-adaptive Gaussian-mixture as the proposal distribution. We further develop bootstrap-based confidence intervals. We establish consistency and asymptotic normality of the maximum likelihood estimator, validate the method in simulations, and illustrate it on single-cell RNA-seq, Alzheimer's Disease Neuroimaging Initiative (ADNI), and TCGA-BRCA data. These applications illustrate uncertainty quantification and interpretable soft memberships, ranging from imbalanced cell populations to graded compatibility with AD-like and non-AD-like prototypes.
