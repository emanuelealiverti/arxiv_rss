Reported accuracy in Parkinson's disease (PD) machine learning can reflect the evaluation protocol rather than real signal. We ask how much evaluation choices inflate performance by comparing naive and rigorous protocols on voice, telemonitoring and blood-transcriptomic datasets. Subject-aware splitting reduced voice AUROC from 0.892 to 0.780 and telemonitoring R^2 from 0.105 to -0.037; Random Forest recovered neither gap. Supervised probe selection before splitting inflated transcriptomic AUROC from 0.711 to 0.998. A nested 50-probe panel matched a 1,000-probe panel (0.811 versus 0.844), and recruitment-wave batch structure was detected. Future work should validate a stable panel on an independent cohort.
