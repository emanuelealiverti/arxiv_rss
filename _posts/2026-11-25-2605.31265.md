Intertemporal choice is usually summarized by a discount rate or a predefined parametric discount function, leaving differences in the shape of individual discounting over the horizon unobserved. This paper proposes a Functional Data Analysis framework that reconstructs, for each respondent, a choice-implied subjective-time trajectory $\tau_i(t)$ from monetary equivalence judgments collected at thirteen horizons between seven days and five years. Equivalence judgments are mapped into discount factors, regularized by monotone shape-constrained smoothing, and converted into trajectories under the representation $f_i(t)=\exp\{-k_i\tau_i(t)\}$, with $k_i$ an operational scale. The trajectories are analyzed through derivatives, functional principal component analysis and clustering of the component scores, compared with exponential, Weber-Fechner and Stevens benchmarks in sample and in a leave-one-horizon-out design, and related to an explicit subjective-time positioning task. A Monte Carlo study evaluates the recovery of trajectory shape and group structure. The empirical application uses an online multilingual survey with 141 respondents; 140 are eligible, 61 report the full future amount at every horizon and 19 provide profiles that do not determine a decreasing discount curve, leaving 60 identified trajectories. The mean trajectory is predominantly concave, two functional components account for 92.0% of the variability of the centered trajectories, and four profiles that differ in when subjective time accumulates relative to the mean are identified; the profiles also differ in discounting intensity. Parametric subjective-time models are competitive in predicting held-out judgments, while partitions based on their parameters do not reproduce the functional partition. No statistically detectable correspondence with the explicit positioning task is found.
