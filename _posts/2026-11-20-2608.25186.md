Robustness to extreme observations does not guarantee correct quantile targeting or valid uncertainty quantification. We investigate these distinct objectives using a mixture of an asymmetric Laplace density and its log-Pareto scale mixture (AL--LPAL), combining two-sided log-regularly varying tails with a logarithmically unbounded center. We establish posterior propriety for every finite sample and finite coefficient posterior moments of all positive orders. Although the working density preserves its nominal quantile, misspecification can displace the posterior intercept target. Under jointly increasing sample size and contamination magnitude, allowing a nonvanishing contamination fraction and an unknown mixture weight, a draw-wise residual-quantile correction restores the uncontaminated target. We derive the local covariance structure induced by the logarithmic singularity and establish corrected-posterior contraction at rate $n^{-1/3}(\log n)^{-2/3}$. At this scale, the corrected posterior collapses around a random center with a Gaussian-process argmax sampling limit, yielding asymptotically zero coverage of equal-tailed credible intervals for contrasts whose limiting fluctuations have no atom at zero. We develop an interval-calibration procedure combining finite-scale Gaussian-process simulation with Bayesian-bootstrap residual quantiles and establish consistency of the plug-in Gaussian-process reference under specified conditions. Simulations and an airline-delay application demonstrate the practical benefits of targeting correction and uncertainty calibration under extreme contamination.
