Neuroimaging analysis commonly involves identifying task-relevant brain regions and examining functional connectivity. These objectives are typically addressed separately, without a model-based framework. We propose a Bayesian distance-weighted Ising model that jointly quantifies regional importance for task-label prediction and functional connectivity between brain regions. The Ising model favors agreement between interacting labels. We extend it with region-specific interaction parameters and weights based on distances between functional signals. The parameters quantify regional importance for distinguishing tasks, while the weights measure functional connectivity through signal similarity between regions. Inference for this model is challenging because the signals may be observed on unequal time grids and the likelihood function contains an intractable normalizing constant. To address this, we use a basis expansion and an auxiliary variable Markov chain Monte Carlo algorithm. We apply the method to functional near-infrared spectroscopy data from younger and older adults, comparing resting-state and task-related activity. The model reveals age-dependent patterns of regional importance and connectivity. Among younger adults, the left orbitofrontal cortex contributes more strongly to task discrimination than the dorsolateral prefrontal and frontopolar cortices, whereas regional differences are less evident among older adults. Older adults also exhibit greater bilateral connectivity between corresponding regions, consistent with reduced hemispheric lateralization.
