Extreme weather events are critical for climate impact assessments. While physical models can simulate unprecedented extremes, for example, through ensemble boosting, this approach is computationally demanding, often restricting output to coarse spatial resolutions. Probabilistic generative downscaling offers a solution by enabling efficient sampling from the entire climate distribution, including its extreme tails. Here, we first demonstrate that machine learning generated regional climate model heatwaves over Paris are physically realistic. We then leverage an emulated machine learning dataset to perform analog-based downscaling of coarse global climate model ensemble boosting events. Our hybrid framework effectively bridges high-resolution regional detail with rare-event sampling, providing an efficient tool for extreme event downscaling and capturing multi-model uncertainty.
