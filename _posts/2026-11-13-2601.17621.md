summary: Introduces prior‑free credible intervals and Bayes factors by conditioning

This work shows that there are procedures that maintain the subjective Bayesian property of coherently informing decision making under uncertainty (i.e., if they tell you to take a certain bet, you should) without needing to assign a prior distribution over nuisance parameters, only over the parameter(s) of interest. We first carefully define this sense of 'credibility', and show that within this framework one can condition on any statistic of choice rather than only on the full dataset. This relaxation of credibility is a natural middle ground between Bayesian and frequentist statistics in the following sense: To a p% Bayesian credible set we should assign a p% belief after observing both the dataset and the interval, to p% frequentist sets we can generally only assign a p% belief before observing either the data or the interval, while to the statistical sets proposed here we can assign a p% belief after observing the set, but not necessarily after inspecting the full dataset ourselves. We then show that choosing a conditioning statistic whose distribution we know, or can bound, for each value of the parameter of interest leads to prior-free Bayes factors even for composite hypotheses, and to non-parametric credible sets with priors only over the parameter of interest. We derive such procedures for several (groups of) statistical problems. Particularly noteworthy is that any frequentist test gives a Bayes factor of the power over the size of the test, and that anytime-validity of E-process-based tests can be carried over to this Bayes factor.
