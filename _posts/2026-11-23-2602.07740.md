The paper compares two independent von Mises populations relative to a known preferred direction. An angular Fisher information calibration represents each population as a point in a Poincar\'{e} disk. A signed score at that point combines the population's mean departure from the medically preferred direction with its concentration. We test equality of scores using separate likelihood fits and an empirical sandwich standard error. At every fixed pair of concentrations the centered statistic is asymptotically standard normal, including when either population is aligned with or opposed to the target. The resulting test and confidence interval concern the Busemann score difference. Simulations show that rejection under the score null can exceed the nominal level at the smallest sample size and is closer to nominal at larger sizes. Proposed method has larger empirical power for alternative with score contrast, compared to the existing one. An application compares the transformed postoperative astigmatism axes for the SNARE and irrigating VECTIS procedures. The estimated score is smaller for SNARE, but the confidence interval for the score difference includes zero.
