When a core player is ruled out before an NBA game, do teammates whose statistical role resembles that of the removed set (every rotation player ruled out, about three per case) play more? Using injury reports stamped at least six hours before tip-off and rosters reconstructed after the season, we study 2,441 team-games from 2023-24 to 2025-26 in which a core player was ruled out for a medical reason. Among teammates in the same game, one within-case standard deviation more similarity in role profile (three-point attempts, assists and rebounds per minute) goes with about 0.63 more minutes, holding reference minutes and headroom fixed (p < 0.0001); this compares teammates within a game, not the minutes gained because of the absence. The pattern holds in two earlier seasons and in exploratory within-player and without-and-with comparisons. Whether similarity adds to listed position is not settled. The association is small and yielded no statistically detectable reduction in the error of the retrospective minute forecasts we tested. Role coverage, how much of the missing minutes and roles the remaining rotation could restore, showed no consistent incremental association with team role output or the final margin.
