Auxiliary information can improve estimation precision under probability sampling, but the relationship between the study and auxiliary variables may not be known well enough to specify suitable balancing variables. Distributionally balanced designs address this by targeting agreement between sample and population auxiliary distributions. We extend energy-based distributional balance to prescribed unequal inclusion probabilities by comparing the empirical sample distribution with an inclusion-probability-weighted population distribution. Under a regularity condition relating the study values divided by their inclusion probabilities to the auxiliaries, we establish an upper bound on the design variance of the Horvitz-Thompson estimator in terms of the expected energy distance. This bound motivates an algorithm that exchanges units between randomly paired candidate samples without increasing their average energy distance. Combined with random initialization and uniform selection of a final candidate, the procedure preserves the prescribed first-order inclusion probabilities. We evaluate its auxiliary-space properties and estimation performance using generated populations and the Meuse data.
