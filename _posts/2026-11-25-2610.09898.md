summary: CLARA learns Gaussian joint predictive means and covariances for multivariate

Quantifying uncertainty in derived quantities requires forecasts that capture cross-variable dependence. From station observations alone, CLARA predicts the mean and full covariance of a Gaussian joint distribution in one forward pass per lead, trains on a CPU and recalibrates the covariance without altering correlations. On two nationwide Korean weather networks, ten regions on six continents and a nationwide air-quality network, CLARA outperforms learned comparators in lead-mean energy score, by 5.2-5.5% against comparably sized iTransformers with the same temporal-phase inputs on the Korean networks, with gains that persist in two held-out seasons. We prove that the same lower bound on the Cholesky diagonal admits different covariances in different output coordinates. Changing only these coordinates lowers the pressure-difference continuous ranked probability score by 40-67%, lets the learned correlations outperform a fixed correlation matrix in joint likelihood at every lead and, on an electricity grid, improves the joint likelihood with two other backbones.
