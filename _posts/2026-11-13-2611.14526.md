Survey estimates for population segments, such as media use by sex and age, rest on few respondents per cell, and a growing literature offers synthetic respondents generated by large language models to fill the gap. That literature has not tested them against the small-area estimators a methodologist would otherwise use. We ran ten estimators, preregistered, on identical household-clustered splits of a Korean media panel across five calibration budgets and three holdout regimes. A multilevel partial-pooling model of the MRP family led every calibration-only estimator at every budget, matching at one hundred respondents what direct estimation reaches with roughly four times as many. On unobserved segments, information fixed as a per-segment prior was inert for synthetic panel and previous wave alike; attached as a bias correction it transferred, at the price of estimation noise. No registered synthetic advantage over MRP survived the paired decision rule. Entered instead as a covariate inside the multilevel model, an exploratory hybrid, the same panel improved on MRP on unobserved segments: the information survives where the registered constructions did not. Interval coverage stayed below nominal throughout and worsened with budget for partial pooling, contrary to prediction.
