Parameter estimates of stochastic actor-oriented models for network evolution are notoriously hard to interpret. Especially a comparison of parameter estimates is problematic, both between different models for the same data set and between the same model for different data sets. Effect size measures based on the calculation of marginal effects are proposed as an alternative to standardize the scale of interpretation but can and should not universally erase the effects of differences in scale and heterogeneity. Simulation experiments show how an omitted effect displaces coefficients and marginal effects differently, and an analysis of empirical friendship network data finds that reciprocity and triadic closure reinforce one another when ties are created but partially substitute for one another when ties are maintained, a contrast the coefficients do not reveal.
