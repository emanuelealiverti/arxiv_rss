The multiple regression quadratic assignment procedure (MRQAP) is a standard framework for inference on dyadic network data, and its properties are well established for the linear model. Network outcomes are often counts, modelled by Poisson regression with a log link. We prove that the invariance property of the Frisch--Waugh--Lovell theorem, the only part that double semi-partialling (DSP) requires, survives the nonlinear link. The DSP focal coefficient and its model-based, HC3 and dyadic cluster-robust standard errors coincide with those of the fit on the raw covariates. The choice between relabelling the raw focal array ($X$-permutation) and relabelling its DSP residual acts only on the reference distribution. The test holds its level when the relabelled designs reproduce the dependence that the statistic retains, and DSP reproduces the autocorrelation of the partialled focal covariate. When the raw focal array and its residual share their autocorrelation, the relabelling test holds the nominal level for all three statistics, at covariate correlations up to $0.9$ and in networks of $20$ to $100$ actors. A normal reference rejects a true null at rates up to $0.96$. With sender and receiver effects modelled, every statistic rejects a true null at $0.035$ to $0.070$ in all conditions, and power against a focal effect of $0.10$ rises from at most $32\%$ to at least $99.9\%$. When the actor effects cannot be modelled, adding the sender and receiver means of the nuisance covariates keeps DSP at its level. We give recommendations and illustrate the method on the EIES message-count network.
