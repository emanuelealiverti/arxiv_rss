Time series of random objects, such as covariance matrices, probability distributions, and functional data, call for forecasting methods that do not rely on arithmetic operations. We introduce geodesic exponential smoothing for time series in Hadamard spaces. The forecast level moves a fixed fraction of the way along the geodesic towards each new observation. The smoothing parameter is estimated by minimising the average squared distance between observations and forecasts. The method involves a single scalar parameter, assumes no stationarity, and updates online in constant time per observation. We further introduce an innovations mechanism under which each observation has conditional Fr\'echet mean equal to the current level, the metric-space analogue of the innovations state-space model. We show via the quasilinearisation of Hadamard spaces that the level is a martingale in a nonlinear sense, that the spread of the data from the initial state grows at least linearly in time, and that their quasi-autocorrelation at any fixed lag tends to one. We prove almost-sure consistency and asymptotic normality of the smoothing-parameter estimator. Three real-data applications, spanning covariance-matrix, distributional, and functional time series, assess the forecasting performance against structurally heavier alternatives.
