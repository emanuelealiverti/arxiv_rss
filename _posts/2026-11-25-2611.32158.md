Nonparametric change-point methods for high-dimensional and non-Euclidean data, including networks and covariance matrices, compare observations through a representation: coordinates, kernels, distances, similarity graphs, ranks, or learned scores. These representations affect which changes a method can detect and what the reported evidence means. We review retrospective methods by relating their representations to the population discrepancies targeted by their scores, the search over change times, the calibration of that search, and the resulting statistical guarantee. We distinguish population identification, detection power, scan-level error control, and localization, and examine these guarantees for multiple changes, dependent sequences, objects estimated from replicates, and online monitoring. Simulation experiments illustrate these distinctions: two scores on the same nearest-neighbor graph have similar power but give different location estimates. These comparisons guide the choice of a representation that preserves the change of scientific interest and a procedure whose guarantee supports the intended inference.
