Seasonal autoregressive integrated moving-average (SARIMA) models typically target the conditional mean of a time series under a Gaussian error assumption. This approach may be inadequate when the innovations are asymmetric because the conditional mean no longer represents the most likely outcome. We propose the Modal SARIMA$(p,d,q)\times(P,D,Q)_s$ model, which links the conditional mode, rather than the conditional mean, to a multiplicative seasonal ARIMA structure. The innovations follow the Fernandez-Steel class of skewed distributions (SKD), whose location parameter is the mode, and the non-seasonal Modal ARIMA$(p,d,q)$ model is the special case without seasonal terms. We give the conditional log-likelihood for the Skew-Normal, Skewed Student-$t$, and Skewed Laplace members, establish the stationarity and invertibility conditions on the product polynomials, show that the recursive modal forecast is the joint mode of the future trajectory, and characterize the correction that yields the marginal $h$-step conditional mode. Monte Carlo experiments show that conditional maximum likelihood and its Wald inference are reliable when the seasonal factors are well separated, that the test of symmetry holds its level from about 200 observations, and that the modal estimator of the dynamic coefficients is as efficient as Gaussian least squares under symmetry and 1.2 to 2.6 times more efficient under asymmetry or heavy tails. We also show, analytically and by simulation, that nearly cancelling seasonal factors amplify the effect of the starting values, and that beyond one step the recursive forecast must be corrected to remain a forecast of the most probable value. In an application to the Canadian lynx trappings, a seasonal component for the ten-year cycle improves the non-seasonal fit for every member of the SKD family. The methods are implemented in the R package ModalForecast.
