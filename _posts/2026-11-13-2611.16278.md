The missing-at-random assumption underlies most analyses of incomplete data. Under nonmonotone missingness, it is especially stringent, imposing pattern-specific restrictions with different conditioning sets. A strictly weaker normalization condition is known to justify maximizing the observed-data likelihood. We show that every law satisfying this condition has a unique missing-at-random counterpart sharing its observed-data and complete-data distributions. Observed-data procedures developed under missing at random for identified functionals, including doubly robust estimators, therefore remain valid, with the same semiparametric efficiency bound. We then prove that the condition holds if and only if filling in the missing values from their conditional distribution under the true full model preserves the complete-data distribution, hence the name completion-invariant missingness. We use a characteristic kernel to compare the completed distribution with the fitted model and assess the joint compatibility of the specified full model and completion-invariant missingness. We derive the asymptotic null distribution with estimated parameters and establish bootstrap and multiplier calibration. Since a parametric full model alone need not identify completion-invariant missingness, we give identification conditions for Gaussian, Student $t$ and finite mixture families. Simulations assess sensitivity to departures from each assumption, and applications to clinical laboratory measurements and childhood growth illustrate the diagnostic.
