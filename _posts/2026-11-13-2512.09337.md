summary: Introduces sequential balancing weights to estimate causal mediation effects

Causal mediation analysis decomposes the average treatment effect into components that operate through intermediate variables and a component that does not. This paper studies weighting estimation of generalized mediation functionals, whose contrasts include natural direct and indirect effects, for any fixed finite number of causally ordered mediators. Inverse propensity score weights are vulnerable to (a) weight instability, because they are products of estimated probability ratios, and (b) finite-sample covariate and mediator imbalance, which can bias the estimator. We instead propose sequential balancing weights, which address (a) by minimizing weight dispersion and (b) by imposing exact balance. Stage one balances covariates between a treatment group and the full sample. Each later stage adds the next mediator and balances a treatment group against the preceding weighted group. A finite-sample bias decomposition of the augmented weighting estimator determines these variables and targets. When exact balance cannot be attained in a small sample, the procedure relaxes the balancing conditions by tolerances selected without using the outcome. Under the stated assumptions, the estimated weights converge at every stage to their population inverse-probability counterparts, with errors from preceding stages entering additively. We also establish asymptotic normality and semiparametric efficiency of the augmented weighting estimator, together with conditions for consistent variance estimation. In the main simulation design with two ordered mediators, the proposed weights outperform the compared methods in bias, root mean squared error, and coverage. In a small media-framing experiment with two ordered mediators, the proposed weights reduce the imbalance left by propensity-score weighting.
