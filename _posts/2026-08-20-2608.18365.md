Bayesian posteriors can converge under model refinement even when the exact sensitivities used by gradient-based samplers do not. We study this mismatch for discretely scale-invariant rough potentials and its consequences for Metropolized Hamiltonian proposals. For Weierstrass truncations, adjacent classical-force increments grow geometrically in the rough regime, whereas fixed additive quotients converge uniformly and fixed multiplicative quotients converge locally away from the origin. The matched Jackson quotient is distinguished not by convergence alone but by dilation covariance and exact finite closure at the intrinsic scale. Uniform negative-log-likelihood approximation is shown to imply explicit total-variation, Hellinger, and bounded quantity-of-interest convergence, with a Gaussian forward-map criterion for Bayesian inverse problems. At the algorithmic level, measurable kick--drift--kick maps are triangular shears, so exact Metropolis correction does not require differentiability of the proposal field. Local uniform field convergence yields convergence of fixed-length proposals, acceptance functions, and bounded-Lipschitz kernels on common compact sets, while the first classical HMC half-kick has no fixed-step refinement limit. Numerical experiments verify the predicted force-refinement rates, show that posterior stabilization can coexist with severe resolution-dependent retuning of exact-gradient HMC, and demonstrate forward convergence without sensitivity convergence in two-dimensional Darcy flow. The computations are used to assess refinement consistency rather than to claim a universal efficiency ordering among proposal fields.
