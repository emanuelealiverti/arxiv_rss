In a longitudinal study, the number of participants and the number of waves both affect how much power the study has to detect a lagged effect. The random intercept cross-lagged panel model (RI-CLPM) is widely used to study within-person dynamics, but existing approaches to power analysis for this model typically rely on simulation or target likelihood-ratio tests. Starting from the likelihood score, we derive analytical Wald power for each lagged coefficient in the bivariate RI-CLPM. Across 30 designs, we compared our analytical results with power estimates from two R packages, powRICLPM for simulated Wald power and semPower for analytical likelihood-ratio power. The mean absolute difference was less than one percentage point in each comparison. By avoiding repeated simulation and model fitting, the analytical method saves time when evaluating power for a target path across many combinations of sample size (N) and number of waves (T).
