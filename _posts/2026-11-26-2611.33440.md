In causal inference with observational data new inferential targets arise when treatment is continuous rather than discrete. Here we form (simultaneous) confidence bands over the continuous treatment effect curve. We form confidence bands based on assuming the curve satisfies shape constraints of either monotonicity or concavity, under the assumption of no unmeasured confounding. As is commonly the case, two nuisance functions arise, and our bands are asymptotically valid if the product of the error rates of the two nuisance functions is small enough. We also develop non-causal shape constrained bands in regression with fixed or random non-uniform grid which are of independent interest. Furthermore, our bands are adaptive to a range of function flatness and smoothness levels in that when the true flatness/smoothness level is unknown the bands still achieve the same (optimal) rate of convergence (in sup-norm) as when the true smoothness level is known. We demonstrate these bands through simulations and an application to the effect of nurse staffing on hospital performance.
