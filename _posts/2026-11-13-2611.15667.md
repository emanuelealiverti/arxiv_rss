Unequal recovery from pandemic learning losses raises concerns about widening achievement gaps between economically disadvantaged students and their peers. This study seeks to identify districts whose mathematics gaps widened in every annual interval from 2022 to 2025. The analysis uses estimates from the Stanford Education Data Archive (SEDA) for 5,600 U.S. school districts. Identifying such districts requires accounting for correlated errors and differing precision in the estimated annual changes, as well as multiplicity. The true annual gap changes in each district form an effect vector, and persistent widening means that every component is positive. To address this class of multiple testing problems, we propose an empirical Bayes mirror procedure. It uses an estimated distribution of effect vectors to guide ranking and covariance-adjusted mirror observations to calibrate selection. The procedure achieves finite-sample false discovery rate (FDR) control under a Gaussian observation model, without requiring accurate estimation of the posterior null probabilities used for ranking. Simulations demonstrate power gains from incorporating information about the distribution of effect vectors. At a target FDR of 25%, the SEDA analysis identifies eight districts in five states with evidence of widening gaps in every annual interval.
