Valid inferential models (IMs) provide frequentist calibration guarantees, but validity alone does not determine which construction is most informative. We study finite-sample efficiency through concentration risk, the expected volume under a possibility contour. For a smooth, fully identified association and a specified parameter-volume measure, a weighted Neyman--Pearson argument identifies the generalized fiducial density as the optimal score for minimizing cut volume subject to fixed-data auxiliary compatibility. The resulting maximum-specificity transform gives an explicit volume interpretation of the fiducial efficiency ceiling, which need not itself be sampling valid. We develop two approaches to valid construction. The first minimizes design-averaged concentration risk among single, data-independent auxiliary-compatible contours. The design measure prioritizes efficiency without requiring prior beliefs or restricting validity. The second localizes the parameter-volume score to candidate-specific contours, yielding a normalized, valid IM equivalent to sampling validification of the relative fiducial likelihood. A parallel sample-space construction recovers the ordinary likelihood-ratio IM; direct validification of the ceiling provides another repair. For the same association, exact, data-independent predictive contours share a common fiducial distribution as a saturating inner probability, while generating different calibrated confidence-region geometries. Design averaging thus selects an efficient member without changing the underlying fiducial law. Examples in a two-component measurement model and an exponential scale model illustrate the efficiency gains and show that sampling-valid repair can improve on the raw ceiling by leaving the fixed-data auxiliary-compatible class over which it is optimal.
