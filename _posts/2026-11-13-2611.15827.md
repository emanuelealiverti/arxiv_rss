Learning heterogeneity from high-dimensional data has become a fundamental task across many data-driven fields. Cluster analysis is a widely used unsupervised tool for this purpose, and many existing methods have been adapted to high-dimensional settings through sparsity constraints. In contrast to algorithmic approaches, model-based clustering offers clean theoretical guaranties and computational simplicity, but can be restrictive in practice due to its reliance on specific parametric distributions. We propose a novel latent semiparametric mixture model for clustering, in which the observed data follow a Gaussian mixture after latent coordinate-wise monotone transformations. Furthermore, assuming a sparse optimal clustering rule in the latent space, we develop a new method, CESME, tailored for high-dimensional clustering. By allowing unspecified transformations, our framework becomes substantially more flexible and robust than traditional model-based approaches. The clustering error of CESME is shown to approach the optimal clustering error at a rate matching that obtained when the true transformations are known. Extensive simulations and real data analysis confirm that CESME outperforms a broad range of existing high-dimensional clustering methods, including those designed for single-cell RNA sequencing data. The online supplementary file contains technical proofs, additional discussions, and more numerical results.
