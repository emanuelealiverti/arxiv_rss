Predictive stability modeling is widely used in pharmaceutical development to establish product shelf-life by identifying the kinetic parameters that govern degradation behavior and using these parameters to extrapolate out in time. Recent approaches typically assume linear degradation dynamics and rely on the Arrhenius equation to model temperature-specific reaction rates. However, observed degradation profiles may reflect nonlinear reaction dynamics and more complex temperature-specific reaction rates, potentially leading to reduced accuracy in long-term predictions and motivating a more flexible approach. We propose a novel Bayesian framework which extends predictive stability modeling beyond simple Arrhenius low-order kinetic dynamics and accommodates complex degradation mechanisms. The degradation process is modeled through the Sestak-Berggren kinetic formulation, a flexible ordinary differential equation encompassing many classical degradation models as special cases. To facilitate model interpretability and model exploration, probabilistic model selection on kinetic parameters automatically determines whether nonlinear reaction components are supported by the data, allowing the model to adaptively collapse to simpler dynamics when appropriate. To address departure from classical Arrhenius kinetics, we develop a generalized temperature reaction rate formulation in which temperature-specific reaction rates are shrunk toward Arrhenius predictions through a hierarchical shrinkage prior while allowing departures when supported by the data. This approach yields robustness to misspecification and reduces sensitivity to potentially influential high-temperature observations commonly encountered in accelerated stability experiments. The resulting framework provides a principled and flexible statistical tool for model-informed shelf-life determination.
