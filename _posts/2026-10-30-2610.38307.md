We develop a robust quadratic score test for a specified mean function in functional and longitudinal data. The construction combines a convex loss with subject-level scores while retaining dependence among repeated measurements. An explicit score-centering condition ensures that the target remains the population mean. We establish the null distribution and Sobolev separation rates under sparse and dense sampling, identifying a transition from a nonparametric testing rate to the root-n boundary. A Gaussian multiplier bootstrap remains valid across these regimes, including settings with non-Gaussian quadratic limits. For implementation, we use a finite spline approximation and estimate the curvature through symmetric score increments. Under the stated conditions, the feasible procedure preserves null calibration and attains a consistency separation rate matching the lower bound over model classes containing a Gaussian submodel. Simulations illustrate the performance of squared, Huber, and absolute losses under regular errors and contamination, and an analysis of longitudinal CD4 counts demonstrates the method.
