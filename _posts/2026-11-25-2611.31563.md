Multiverse analysis has been proposed as a means to transparently communicate the influence of methodological decisions and their combinations on empirical outcomes. However, scholars warn that the method can be misleading when the methodological decisions are de-emphasized and the wide distribution of estimates from various decisions is interpreted out of context as ammunition to attack social science research for being non-robust. The potential for the misuse of multiverse analysis is exacerbated by the weaknesses of the current reporting methods of multiverse analysis, mostly via the two mainstream ways of data visualization: density plot and specification curve. The former completely neglects methodological choices, while the latter becomes messy even with only a few methodological decisions, as the number of elements to be plotted at least doubles when one decision is added. In this article, we propose a visualization method that is based on pruning a full decision tree, such that the most important decisions remain. The visualization can communicate what methodological decision combinations lead to systematic changes in estimates inside a multiverse. Our visualization scales well even when many methodological decisions are considered and avoids common misinterpretations of existing reporting standards. We argue that multiverse analysis and its visualizations should be considered as an opportunity to obtain a deeper understanding of how an empirical outcome is generated out of many methodological decisions. Our proposed visualization method fulfills this purpose and communicate robustness of empirical findings in an equitable manner.
