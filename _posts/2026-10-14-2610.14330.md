Treatment decisions are often sequential and depend on information absent from the recorded history. Latent sequential ignorability models such assignment by assuming that baseline covariates and principal strata, defined by joint potential intermediate responses, account for confounding of later treatment. For two treatment periods with a binary intermediate response, we derive sharp population and principal effect bounds under a specified association between the potential responses and show that the two targets can require different optimizations to obtain sharp bounds. For population effects, optimization over latent treatment probabilities reduces to their feasible endpoints. Stronger selection between response types can be offset by making their outcome distributions more similar, preserving both the observed distribution and population marginal outcome distributions. This replacement can change effects within principal strata, whose sharp bounds may instead require interior treatment probabilities. Restrictions on differences in conditional effects can tighten the bounds without equating outcome levels. For binary outcomes, we give an exact finite algorithm that includes these interior assignments. We also develop inference under smoothness and estimation conditions. We illustrate the bounds in a study of gym attendance after an incentive program.
