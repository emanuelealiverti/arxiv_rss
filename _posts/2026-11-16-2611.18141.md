On Euclidean space, the losses that produce consistent M-estimators of a functional are its strictly consistent scoring functions (Dimitriadis, Fissler and Ziegel, 2024), and Osband's principle characterizes these through identification functions, which define method-of-moments estimators. We extend this characterization to the probability simplex. We define the residual, which on Euclidean space is a subtraction, as the inverse exponential map of an affine connection. It identifies the exponential barycenter of the connection: the arithmetic, normalized geometric and Fr\'echet means for the mixture, exponential and Levi-Civita connections. Its integral along geodesics is the canonical divergence of Ay and Amari (2015). A geometric form of Osband's principle decides which losses each connection admits. For the flat connections every strictly convex generator yields a consistent Bregman loss. For the curved $\alpha$-connections, half the squared Fisher-Rao distance is (up to scale) the only consistent loss for the Fr\'echet mean, and for $\alpha \notin \{-1, 0, 1\}$ no such loss exists and only the method of moments remains. The residual is integrable only at $\alpha = 0$, sharpening the integrability claim of Ay and Amari. The characterization yields the estimating equations of regression on the simplex and the calibration decomposition of compositional forecasts.
