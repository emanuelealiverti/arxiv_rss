Incidental parameters can make maximum likelihood inconsistent in short panels. When they act through a known translation group, integration with respect to Haar measure eliminates them exactly and gives an invariant likelihood, whose score is shown to be the conditional expectation, given the maximal invariant, of the Neyman-orthogonalised score of every order. Under interactive fixed effects the orbit is an unknown factor space, and the invariant score for it has a drift equal to minus the derivative of the entropy of the retained contrasts. An entropy adjustment removes this drift and, at the true structural parameter, identifies the factor space uniquely when the loadings span it. The adjusted estimating equations are the gradient of a single criterion if and only if the entropy separates into an orbit term and an error-law term, as in scale and elliptical families; consistency then follows from a design condition, and in general from an equilibrium-identification condition, with asymptotic normality for fixed panel length. A boundary is derived for recovering a skewness sign discarded by a two-period invariant reduction. An application to Vietnamese rice farming rejects equal household-effect weights across survey waves and finds lower returns to scale.
