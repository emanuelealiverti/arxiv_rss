Two-sided marketplace platforms often use randomized experiments to evaluate new features, raising the question of whether to randomize customers or listings. Randomizing the smaller (\emph{short}) side can reduce interference bias due to weaker competition, while randomizing the larger (\emph{long}) side provides a larger nominal sample, potentially reducing variance. On the surface, these competing considerations suggest a bias-variance trade-off in choosing which side to randomize. We investigate this trade-off in a two-sided marketplace with first-come, first-served (FCFS) booking and heterogeneous customer preferences, studying the bias and variance of difference-in-means estimators of the global average treatment effect. Our findings suggest that rather than a bias-variance tradeoff, in fact short-side randomization is generally preferable on {\em both} dimensions. We prove these results in an asymptotic regime where market imbalance increases. We establish conditions under which (1) short-side randomization eliminates first-order interference bias, while long-side randomization retains it; and (2) short-side randomization also achieves a variance rate of no larger asymptotic order than long-side randomization. The key insight is that although the long side contains more participants, the number of matches is constrained by the population on the short side, limiting the apparent variance advantage of randomizing a larger sample. Our conditions accommodate heterogeneous preferences and treatment effects and are satisfied by familiar logit models of customer choice. Together, these findings support randomizing on the short side, despite the larger sample size available on the long side.
