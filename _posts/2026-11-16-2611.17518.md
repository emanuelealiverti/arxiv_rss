We develop a Bayesian semiparametric regression framework for count data that can accommodate both underdispersion and overdispersion without specifying a full parametric probability model. The model is built from the first two conditional factorial moments of a zero altered distribution. Covariates may enter the conditional mean and the conditional dispersion separately, thereby allowing the dispersion to vary across observations. We turn the moment restrictions into an adjusted exponentially tilted empirical likelihood (AETEL) criterion and combine this criterion with Gaussian priors to form a Bayesian empirical-likelihood pseudo-posterior. The adjustment improves numerical feasibility and a two-block Metropolis delayed acceptance sampler allows for faster runs of the MCMC chains. This reduces unnecessary exact evaluations without changing the AETEL target. Using existing Bayesian exponentially tilted empirical likelihood (ETEL) theory, we state a first-order posterior normal approximation and verify the moment, smoothness, and Jacobian rank conditions needed for the asymptotic inference. Simulations under zero inflated Poisson and Conway Maxwell Poisson data exhibit small bias, accurate uncertainty estimates, and near nominal interval coverage for both overdispersed and underdispersed counts. Applications to various datasets illustrate how the Bayesian analysis can identify covariate dependent dispersion using finite sample posterior inference.
