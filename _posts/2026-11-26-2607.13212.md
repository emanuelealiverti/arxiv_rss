Estimating an unknown cumulative distribution function (cdf) is fundamental both as a statistical goal and as an input to downstream optimization. In practice, however, distribution estimation is often complicated by incomplete knowledge of the distribution's structure and limited, censored data. We address the first challenge by studying distributions with failure-rate shape constraints, especially increasing failure rate (IFR), without assuming a fully specified parametric family. To address the second, we consider noisy quantile data: at finitely many prespecified knots, each observation records only whether an independent sample lies below or above the knot. This setting arises naturally in pricing, reliability, and healthcare. We formulate the IFR-constrained maximum likelihood estimator and show that the original problem is infinite-dimensional and non-convex. Our two-step approach solves a finite-dimensional convex optimization problem over transformed knot values, then reconstructs a full cdf through shape-preserving interpolation. We establish finite-sample error bounds and convergence rates that guide offline data collection. We also show how the framework accommodates additional properties: (i) decreasing failure rate, (ii) failure rate average, (iii) generalized failure rate, and (iv) new better than used. Numerical experiments and case studies in revenue management and reliability illustrate improved goodness of fit and downstream decision quality.
