The increasing availability of detailed football performance data has created new opportunities for identifying player archetypes and informing recruitment and tactical decision-making. Most existing clustering approaches assign each player to a single group, despite the reality that many modern footballers perform hybrid roles spanning multiple stylistic categories. Performance data for 314 outfield players from the 2024/25 English Premier League season are analysed using a Gaussian mixture model (GMM) applied to principal component representations of player statistics. Unlike hard-assignment methods such as k-means, the GMM provides probabilistic cluster memberships, allowing uncertainty and role hybridity to be quantified. Seven player archetypes are identified in the full dataset. Posterior membership probabilities reveal a subset of players whose profiles span multiple clusters, providing an objective measure of tactical flexibility and stylistic ambiguity. Further analyses within defender, midfielder, and forward groups recover position-specific sub-archetypes and highlight exceptional outliers. The results demonstrate that model-based clustering provides a flexible and interpretable framework for identifying latent structure in high-dimensional performance data while explicitly quantifying uncertainty in cluster membership. More broadly, the approach is readily transferable to other sports and domains in which individuals exhibit overlapping characteristics and cannot be adequately represented by hard cluster assignments alone.
