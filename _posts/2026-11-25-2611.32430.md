Many real-world interactions involve groups of nodes rather than pairs. A non-uniform hypergraph records such interactions as hyperedges of different orders, where the order counts the nodes in a hyperedge. Full estimation evaluates every candidate hyperedge, whose number grows exponentially with the order, and classical remedies sacrifice higher-order information or aggravate computation. The orders are not isolated: the lower orders, cheap to compute and rich in information, reveal where higher-order hyperedges are likely to occur. We propose CINE (Cross-order Information-guided Negative-sampling Estimation) under a latent space model in which all orders share latent positions. Starting from order two, CINE climbs one order at a time. At each higher order, CINE keeps all present hyperedges, samples a small subset of negative examples guided by lower-order scores, and corrects for the known inclusion probabilities to preserve the target of full estimation. CINE thereby lets users choose the trade-off between computation time and estimation accuracy: the retained orders and the sampling budget set the time, and the retained information provably approaches full-data information as the sampling budget grows. In simulations, CINE approaches the accuracy of full estimation at a small fraction of its computation time, and larger teams in MADStat reveal research associations.
