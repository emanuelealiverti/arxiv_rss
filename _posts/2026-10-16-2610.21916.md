Statistical modeling of high-dimensional and large-sample data with complex correlation structures is increasingly important in biomedical research, particularly in large-scale omics applications such as genomic studies. Analyzing these large-scale datasets imposes substantial computational and memory demands, requiring efficient model training strategies that maintain predictive accuracy. We propose Prescreen, Then Subsample (PTS), a two-stage approach that combines variable selection with optimal-design-based informative subsampling to address both high dimensionality and large sample size. The first stage screens informative variables and can explicitly account for covariate correlation; the second stage selects informative observations in the prescreened variable space. In contrast to existing two-stage subdata selection approaches, PTS can incorporate multivariable fine-mapping to identify a correlation-aware covariate space for informative subsampling. In particular, we integrate the Sum of Single Effects (SuSiE) model for fine-mapping-based prescreening with information-based optimal subdata selection (IBOSS) for sample selection. Simulation studies based on correlated Gaussian covariates and biobank-scale synthetic genotype data show that PTS reduces model training time while achieving predictive performance comparable to full-data training. We find that SuSiE-based PTS provides improved predictive performance consistent with better stage-one screening precision compared with commonly used univariate prescreening methods. PTS therefore provides a flexible and scalable training framework for large-scale biomedical data with complex correlation structures, including genomic data with linkage disequilibrium.
