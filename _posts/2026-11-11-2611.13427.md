The prediction market platform Kalshi offers individual markets for the winner in each U.S. congressional district and an aggregate market for the total number of Democratic House seats. We develop an applied statistical framework for reconciling these two market layers, since the aggregate seat count is determined by the collection of individual district outcomes. The framework combines a latent-Gaussian dependence structure with a log-odds adjustment to district probabilities and a power adjustment to aggregate seat-count probabilities, with parameters estimated by divergence minimization. Applied to a July 30, 2026 snapshot, the three-parameter primary model achieves close reconciliation, with forward and reverse KL divergences both approximately 0.0013, while yielding a positive latent national correlation, a negative district log-odds shift, and flattening of the aggregate target distribution. Daily analysis of 236 dates from January through August 2026 reveals two relatively stable periods separated by a spring transition period exhibiting substantial volatility in both parameter and divergence values. The framework provides a diagnostic approach for assessing cross-market consistency in hierarchically structured prediction or financial markets.
