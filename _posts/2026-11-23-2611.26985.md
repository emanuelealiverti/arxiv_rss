Multilayer networks on a common node set, such as brain connectomes of several animals with one layer per animal, often share a low-dimensional geometry but differ in how strongly each layer expresses it and in node activity. We study a multilayer latent space network model that combines an invariant subspace common to all layers with layer-specific interaction matrices and node-by-layer degree effects. By introducing the generalized Cayley transform of the invariant subspace, we characterize the intrinsic geometry of the model and obtain sharp convergence rates for the maximum likelihood estimator, asymptotic normality and Neyman orthogonality for vertex-level parameters, and asymptotic normality for the interaction matrices with a non-negligible bias. Plug-in estimates of the bias and covariance give debiased confidence regions and tests of equality between layers. We also propose an easy-to-implement one-step estimator that attains the same vertex-level limits. In a real-world mouse connectome dataset, the inbred strain that lacks a corpus callosum shows about twice the hemispheric segregation of the other three strains.
