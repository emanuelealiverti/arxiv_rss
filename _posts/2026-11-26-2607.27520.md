Longitudinal biomedical studies increasingly collect irregularly sampled, multivariate categorical measurements that are noisy manifestations of underlying continuous disease processes. These data pose several challenges: subjects exhibit heterogeneous progression patterns; multiple observed items reflect unobserved biological traits that may evolve interdependently; and observation schedules often have unequal intervals both within and across subjects. To address these challenges, we present the Continuous Latent Ornstein--Uhlenbeck Dynamics (CLOUD) framework for modeling complex disease trajectories from multivariate longitudinal categorical data. CLOUD links multivariate categorical observations to underlying latent functional domains and characterizes their coupled temporal evolution by integrating item response theory, factor analysis, and multivariate Ornstein--Uhlenbeck (OU) processes. Methodologically, we introduce a time-inhomogeneous OU process that incorporates covariate-dependent progression slopes into the moving mean function of the latent dynamics, allowing baseline biomarkers and clinical characteristics to modulate individual disease trajectories while preserving the analytical tractability of the OU process. We further propose a Lyapunov-based structural parameterization of the joint drift and stationary covariance that enforces valid dynamics by construction and enables efficient Markov chain Monte Carlo inference. We establish well-posedness and conditional identifiability. Under additional regularity conditions, we establish consistency and asymptotic properties of the model. Through simulation studies and an application to Parkinson's Progression Markers Initiative data, we demonstrate that CLOUD can characterize subject-specific disease evolution across multiple interacting functional domains.
