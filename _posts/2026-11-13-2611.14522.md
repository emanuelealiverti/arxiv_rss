Sampling geometry may influence both reconstruction stability and the smoothing needed to control noise. This study distinguishes geometry-dependent failure of exact multiquadric radial basis function (RBF) interpolation from variation in validation-selected smoothing. A synthetic benchmark crossed four response families, four 16-point geometries, three slice levels, five observation regimes, and 24 repeated holdouts, yielding 17,280 method-level evaluations. Clough--Tocher cubic interpolation, exact RBF, and smoothed RBF were compared, followed by a Water Quality Portal pilot with 1,950 complete activities. On the synthetic shared-valid domain, exact RBF achieved the lowest noise-free mean RMSE (0.0206), whereas smoothed RBF performed best under noise (0.1807 versus 0.2414 for cubic and 0.6106 for exact RBF). Clustered-gap support produced the largest noisy exact-RBF RMSE (1.2706). Geometry added little explanatory power for log-transformed smoothing: the combined model had $R^2=0.4259$, compared with 0.4096 for noise alone. Reported failure-regression results suggest a stronger geometry signal, but remain unverified without their original model specification. In the environmental pilot, smoothed RBF achieved mean RMSE 0.9877 with no negative-$R^2$ failures. Conflicting responses at identical predictor coordinates made exact interpolation unsuitable. All environmental smoothed-RBF fits selected the largest candidate, preventing geometry--smoothing correlation analysis. The findings motivate geometry-aware failure screening but do not establish a transferable rule for smoothing magnitude. Independent multisite validation, appropriate coordinate scaling, joint scale--smoothing tuning, and conditioning diagnostics are needed before an adaptive policy can be claimed.
