This article introduces a novel nonparametric test for conditional mean independence, which formally evaluates whether a random vector X has an additional impact on the mean regression function of a response Y when a conditioning random vector Z is already included. By framing the problem as classical partial effect testing, our approach goes beyond traditional parametric models to provide a highly general and flexible framework. The test statistic is constructed using martingale difference divergence (MDD) after neutralizing the effect of Z on Y via a sieve approximation. The population residual MDD characterizes conditional mean independence under suitable moment conditions. We present a theoretical framework for the test under both a fixed number of basis functions and a growing sieve dimension. We derive the nonstandard and non-pivotal asymptotic null distributions in both regimes. To facilitate practical implementation, we propose a wild bootstrap procedure to approximate these limiting distributions and establish its asymptotic consistency. To establish the fundamental properties required for the test to be widely useful, we conduct a power analysis, demonstrating the test's consistency under fixed alternatives and establishing its asymptotic power against local alternatives. The practical utility and finite-sample performance of the proposed test are examined through simulation studies and an exploratory application to a real-world variable selection problem in regression modeling. The code for the proposed test and numerical simulations can be found at: https://github.com/leece087/Conditional-Mean-Independence-Test.
