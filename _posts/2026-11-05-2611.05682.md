We implement the Structured Functional Principal Component Analysis (SFPCA) framework of Shou et al. (2015) in a four-level nested setting with arbitrarily unbalanced group sizes at every level of the hierarchy. While the SFPCA framework can in principle accommodate deeper hierarchical structures, practical implementations remain challenging when the number of curves is large and the hierarchy is highly unbalanced. Our contributions are twofold. First, we operationalize SFPCA for four hierarchical levels with arbitrary unbalancedness across units. Second, we propose a memory-efficient computational strategy that makes the analysis of large collections of curves feasible on conventional hardware. In particular, we construct the four design-specific matrices required for the Method of Moments (MoM) estimators of the level-specific covariance operators while controlling their memory footprint, which would otherwise grow prohibitively with the number of curves. We illustrate the approach using three subsets of the NCAA-DoD Grand Alliance Concussion Assessment, Research, and Education (CARE) Consortium dataset (2014-2017).
