Meta-analyses of survival rates require precision information, such as standard errors (SEs), which is often unreported. We previously proposed imputing missing SEs from reported survival rates, sample sizes, and risk-set sizes. The original method has two limitations. First, it replaces Kaplan--Meier estimates of 1 with 0.99 regardless of sample size. Second, when the risk-set size is unavailable, it uses the total event count as a fallback. The count is often unreported, and the fallback can also substantially overestimate SEs. We propose a sample-size-dependent boundary correction based on a pseudo-event and a method that borrows time-specific event information from studies with available risk-set sizes. In simulations, the boundary correction was more stable than fixed replacement, and borrowing reduced study-level SE imputation bias. Borrowing generally improved precision by retaining studies with missing SEs that lacked both risk-set sizes and event counts.
