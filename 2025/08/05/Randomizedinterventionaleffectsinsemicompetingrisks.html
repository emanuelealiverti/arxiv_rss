Randomized interventional effects in semicompeting risks


Yuhao Deng, Rui Wang, Xiang Zhan
[stat.ME]

In clinical studies, the risk of the primary (terminal) event may be modified by intermediate events, resulting in semicompeting risks. To study the treatment effect on the terminal event mediated by the intermediate event, researchers wish to decompose the total effect into direct and indirect effects. In this article, we extend the randomized interventional approach to time-to-event data, where both intermediate and terminal events are subject to right censoring. We envision a random draw for the intermediate event process according to some reference distribution, either marginally over time-varying confounders or conditionally given observed history. We present the identification formula for interventional effects and discuss some variants of the identification assumptions. We estimate the treatment effects using nonparametric maximum likelihood estimation and propose a sensitivity analysis. We study the effect of matched unrelated donor versus haploidentical donor on death mediated by relapse in a hematopoietic cell transplantation study with graft-versus-host disease as the time-varying confounder. We find that matched unrelated donor transplantation is preferable under the use of post-transplantation PTCy GVHD prophylaxis for lymphoma patients.
