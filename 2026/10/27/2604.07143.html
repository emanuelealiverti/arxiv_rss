Lumbermark: Resistant Clustering by Chopping Up Mutual Reachability Minimum Spanning Trees

medium Marek Gagolewski · stat.AP, stat.ML · 27 Oct 2026
Introduces Lumbermark, a divisive clustering method that partitions data by chopping mutual reachability minimum spanning tree limbs.

Abstract

We introduce Lumbermark, a robust divisive clustering algorithm capable of detecting clusters of varying sizes, densities, and shapes. Lumbermark iteratively chops off large limbs connected by protruding segments of a dataset’s mutual reachability minimum spanning tree. The use of mutual reachability distances smoothens the data distribution and decreases the influence of low-density objects, such as noise points between clusters or outliers at their peripheries. The algorithm can be viewed as a divisive version of Genie and an alternative to HDBSCAN that produces partitions with user-specified sizes. A fast, easy-to-use implementation of this new method is available in the open-source lumbermark package for Python and R. We show that Lumbermark performs well on benchmark data and hope it will prove useful to data scientists and practitioners across different fields.
