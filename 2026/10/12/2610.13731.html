confoundvis for Visualizing and Reporting Sensitivity to Unmeasured Confounding in R

low Subir Hait · stat.CO · 12 Oct 2026
A software package for visualizing and reporting sensitivity to unmeasured confounding in R.

Abstract

Sensitivity analysis asks how strong unmeasured confounding would have to be before an observational conclusion changes. Established methods answer this question using different quantities, including partial $R^2$ values, correlation products, and risk ratios. In this article, we present confoundvis, an R package that organizes these results through a common object, plotting functions, and reports. It calculates partial $R^2$ and impact-threshold paths from fitted linear models, imports results from sensemakr, konfound, and EValue, and provides measured-covariate benchmarks for the OLS frameworks. We demonstrate the workflow with 5,735 patients from the right heart catheterization study. The adjusted linear probability coefficient is 0.0634, the point-null robustness value is 0.0608, and the conditional significance impact threshold is 0.0376. The largest adverse leave-one-term-out measured-covariate product is 0.0090. Because the benchmark and threshold use different conditioning sets, their ratio is descriptive. A separate modified Poisson model gives a risk ratio of 1.103 and an E-value of 1.439. The contribution is a software workflow that keeps each method’s units and decision rule clear. The package is available from CRAN.
