Estimand frameworks for causal inference in clinical research and regulatory decision making

low Miguel A. Hernán, Mats J. Stensrud, Xabier García-Albéniz, Issa J. Dahabreh, Jonathan A. C. Sterne · stat.ME · 22 Oct 2026
Three frameworks for causal inference in clinical research are compared and translated.

Abstract

Causal inference for clinical and regulatory decision making requires a precise definition of the causal estimand, that is, what we want to estimate. Three frameworks are commonly used to describe causal estimands: PICO and its extensions, the target trial framework, and the E9(R1) Addendum of the International Council for Harmonisation (ICH). Because these frameworks were developed by different scientific communities, they operate at different levels of detail, emphasize different aspects of the causal estimand, and use different terminology for closely related concepts. Here we compare the three frameworks and translate across their terminologies. PICO is a compact framework that helps jumpstart a conversation about the causal question, but it does not encode sufficient information to fully characterize a causal estimand. The target trial framework characterizes the causal estimand precisely by specifying the components of the protocol of the randomized trial that would answer the causal question: eligibility criteria, treatment strategies, assignment, outcome, start and end of follow-up, and causal contrast. E9(R1) starts from intercurrent events and lists strategies to address them, some of which describe estimators rather than estimands, or imply estimands that are not relevant to decision makers or cannot be estimated under reasonable assumptions. We argue that the target trial framework subsumes the other two, and we provide a map of the correspondences across the three frameworks.
