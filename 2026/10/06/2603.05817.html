Two Localization Strategies for Sequential MCMC Data Assimilation with Applications to Nonlinear Non-Gaussian Geophysical Models

high Hamza Ruzayqat, Hristo G. Chipilski, Omar Knio · stat.CO, stat.AP, stat.ME · 6 Oct 2026
Develops localized data assimilation methods for sequential MCMC in nonlinear non-Gaussian state-space models.

Abstract

We present a localized data assimilation (DA) scheme based on sequential Markov Chain Monte Carlo (SMCMC) \cite{smcmc}, a provably convergent method for filtering high-dimensional, nonlinear, and potentially non-Gaussian state-space models. Unlike particle filters, which are exact for such models, SMCMC does not assign weights to samples, avoiding weight degeneracy in small-ensemble regimes. We design two localization approaches within the SMCMC framework that exploit observation sparsity to reduce effective degrees of freedom. The first variant collects observed blocks into a single reduced domain and runs parallel MCMC chains over this combined region. The second further reduces per-chain state dimension by decomposing the observed region into independent blocks with compact halos, applying Gaspari–Cohn tapering to down-weight distant observations. When the observation model is linear Gaussian, the approximate filtering density reduces to a Gaussian mixture from which independent samples can be drawn exactly; otherwise, we employ an MCMC kernel. We test on high-dimensional ($d \sim 10^4 - 10^5$) state-space models, including a linear Gaussian model and a nonlinear multilayer shallow water equation with linear and nonlinear observation operators. We consider Gaussian and Student-$t$ observation noise, showing that LSMCMC handles heavy-tailed errors that cause ensemble Kalman methods to diverge. Observations include synthetic and real SWOT (NASA) and ocean drifter (NOAA) data. We compare the two variants against each other and the local ensemble transform Kalman filter (LETKF).
