Human-AI Interaction Mediated by Courtship Language Culture: Computational Statistics and Probabilistic Modeling

low Linh Huynh, Nick Cyriax, Jackson George, Stephanie Zoltick, Zachariah Yusaf · stat.AP · 6 Oct 2026
A study of human-AI interaction in a collegiate courtship tradition using computational statistics and probabilistic modeling.

Abstract

Large language models are entering living cultural practices, including intimate ones. We study a collegiate courtship tradition at Dartmouth College called flitzing, the tradition of sending rhymed flirtatious emails to ask peers out, as a measurable microcosm of a culture absorbing artificial intelligence. We combine four sources of evidence: human evaluation surveys in two waves (2025 and 2026); information retrieval over 33 human-written and 5,610 AI-generated flitzes spanning 17 sampling temperatures; evaluation of the same survey by three generative language models; and simulation of a coupled multi-species bipartite spin glass model of people and texts. Four main findings emerge. First, sampling temperature induces a phase transition in diversity: below the transition, AI-generated flitzes are more homogeneous than the human, while above it they decohere, and human baselines of diversity remain stable across years, indicating persistent collective structure in human creativity. Second, human judges conflate perceived quality with human authorship, whereas AI judges detect provenance almost perfectly yet still rate human-written flitzes higher. Third, the transition window, with temperature between 1.25 and 1.38, is consistent across biased survey data, unbiased text statistics, and the mathematical model, which also reproduces the observed clustering. Fourth, detection ability generalizes beyond the local culture, with Dartmouth and non-Dartmouth respondents performing comparably, and strengthens over time, with 2026 respondents above chance at every temperature. We conclude that AI participation in a tight cultural genre is regime dependent: conformist and homogenizing below criticality, detectable and disruptive above it.
