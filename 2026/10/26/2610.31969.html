Proportion Outcomes in Interrupted Time Series Analysis: Why Both Boundedness and Autocorrelation Matter

low Ariel Linden · stat.AP · 26 Oct 2026
This paper examines the importance of accounting for autocorrelation and bounded outcomes in interrupted time series analysis of proportions.

Abstract

Background: Proportions are common in interrupted time series analysis (ITSA), but bounded outcomes and serial dependence require distinct modeling considerations. This article provides guidance on selecting and evaluating models for proportion outcomes. Methods: Two published healthcare ITSA studies were reanalyzed. The first examined weekly proportions near the boundaries of the unit interval using segmented linear regression. The second examined monthly proportions without reported assessment or adjustment for autocorrelation. Both were reanalyzed using models appropriate for their outcome distributions and temporal dependence. Results: In the first study, segmented linear regression produced six fitted values below zero, and 35 of 66 confidence intervals extended beyond the permissible outcome range. Fractional logistic regression constrained fitted means to the unit interval while accommodating zeros and ones. In the second study, beta autoregressive regression identified significant first-order serial dependence (rho1 = 0.263, 95% CI 0.093 to 0.432; P = .002). After adjustment, residuals showed no evidence of remaining autocorrelation (Ljung-Box Q12 = 15.59; P = .211). Conclusions: Boundedness and serial dependence are distinct considerations in ITSA of proportions. Accounting for autocorrelation does not ensure predictions remain within the permissible range, and modeling bounded outcomes does not ensure serial dependence is addressed. Investigators should assess both features and select models appropriate for the outcome distribution and temporal dependence.
