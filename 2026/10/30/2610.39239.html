Bregman projections and partition design for accelerating finite Markov chains

high Michael C. H. Choi, Youjia Wang · stat.CO · 30 Oct 2026
Introduces Bregman projection-based partition design to accelerate finite Markov chain mixing.

Abstract

We study Gibbs averaging of finite Markov chains with a prescribed stationary distribution $\pi$. Given a transition matrix $P$ and the Gibbs kernel $G$ induced by a state-space partition, we prove that $GPG$ is an information projection for every admissible Bregman generator $\phi$, with divergences defined using transition probabilities relative to $\pi$. A Pythagorean identity characterizes this projection and recovers the relative entropy and weighted Frobenius cases. A dual characterization identifies $GPG$ as a kernel closest to stationarity among those with the same projection chain as $P$; for relative entropy, it maximizes the entropy rate. The identities also give monotonicity under partition coarsening. For every reversible ergodic baseline $P$, Gibbs averaging cannot increase the squared weighted Frobenius distance to stationarity at any positive integer time, and the inequality is strict unless $P=GPG$. The corresponding mixing time therefore cannot increase. To choose the partition, we identify the divergence of $G$ from stationarity as the exact worst-case divergence of $GPG$ over all $\pi$-stationary baselines $P$. This objective depends only on the block masses. We give an exact algorithm for concave block costs, including those arising from relative entropy and power generators $\phi(u)=u^p$ with $1 2$. For power generators $\phi(u)=u^p$ with $p>1$, the objective is proportional to Tsallis entropy of order $2-p$: its minimization favors imbalanced block masses when $1 2$, while all $k$-block partitions have the same objective value when $p=2$. A numerical experiment compares the resulting projected chains with a local Metropolis sampler on a multi-modal $\pi$.
