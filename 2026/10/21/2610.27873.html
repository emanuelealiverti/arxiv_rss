Data-Driven Learning of Directed Acyclic Graphs in Gaussian Linear Structural Equation Models with Latent Variables

medium Siliang Zhang, Yunxiao Chen, Irini Moustaki · stat.ME · 21 Oct 2026
A two-stage approach for learning directed acyclic graphs in Gaussian linear structural equation models with latent variables.

Abstract

Structural equation modeling is typically performed in a confirmatory manner, requiring a priori specification of both the measurement model and the path diagram connecting the structural variables. In this paper, we study a semi-exploratory setting within a general Gaussian linear structural equation model with latent variables, where the loading structure in the measurement model is known, but the structural path diagram follows an unknown directed acyclic graph (DAG) to be learned from data. First, we establish the identifiability of the structural model, showing that the unknown DAG is identifiable only up to its Markov equivalence class. We then propose DAGGLE (\underline{D}irected \underline{A}cyclic \underline{G}raph learning in \underline{G}aussian \underline{L}inear structural \underline{E}quation models), a two-stage likelihood-based approach for simultaneous DAG structure learning and parameter estimation. In the first stage, we screen candidate edges via continuous optimization of a penalized marginal likelihood. In the second stage, we further prune the remaining edges using a BIC-guided backward search over the space of Markov equivalence classes. Under mild regularity and search conditions, DAGGLE recovers the true Markov equivalence class with probability tending to one. Finally, we demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach through extensive numerical simulations and an application to the European Social Survey (Trust in Justice module), where DAGGLE yields a model that aligns with substantive theory while improving overall fit.
