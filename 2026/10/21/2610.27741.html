Rerandomization and high-dimensional adjustment with assignment-dependent auxiliary variables under network interference

low Honghao Zhang, Yanghao Wei, Hanzhong Liu · stat.ME · 21 Oct 2026
Proposes rerandomization and covariate adjustment methods for network experiments with interference.

Abstract

Network experiments are widely used to evaluate interventions in settings such as vaccine trials and social networks, where interference is often present. In these settings, predictive auxiliary variables, such as treatment-weighted neighbor summaries, depend on treatment assignments and generally exhibit nonzero exposure effects. In this paper, we propose rerandomization and covariate adjustment methods based on estimation errors in these auxiliary exposure effects to improve precision. For Mahalanobis distance-based rerandomization, we derive the asymptotic distributions of the Horvitz-Thompson and H'ajek estimators under approximate neighborhood interference and quantify the resulting precision gains. At the analysis stage, we propose a covariate adjustment method that acts directly on these estimation errors. This method requires no normalization of the auxiliary variables and is optimal within the corresponding class of linear adjustments. We show that rerandomization and covariate adjustment become asymptotically equivalent as the rerandomization threshold approaches zero, extending this equivalence to settings with interference. For high-dimensional auxiliary variables, we propose Lasso-adjusted estimators and establish their joint asymptotic normality under suitable sparsity conditions. These estimators retain the same asymptotic distribution when combined with fixed-dimensional rerandomization. Simulations and a social-network application illustrate the precision gains from the proposed methods.
