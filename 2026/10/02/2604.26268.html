The Difference Between "Replicable" and "Not Replicable" Is Not Itself Scientifically Replicable

low Erkan O. Buzbas, Berna Devezer · stat.AP, stat.ME · 2 Oct 2026
The paper formalizes two statistical models for replication studies and critiques their limitations.

Abstract

Replication studies estimate the replicability rate of scientific results by aggregating binary verdicts of experiments as replicated'' andnot replicated’’. These estimates assume rarely attainable exact replications. Experiments often do not share a common data-generating process, leading to non-exact replications. We formalize two statistical interpretations of non-exactness. In a shared latent rate model (benchmark), experiments are exchangeable conditional on a common random replicability rate. In a conditionally independent rates model (operational), each experiment has its own random replicability rate, drawn independently from a population. Under the benchmark model, even small variability in assumed replicability rates induces a variance floor of the estimated mean replicability rate that is irreducible by more replications. Under the operational model, the degree of non-exactness is not identifiable from binary verdict data, which contain no information about between-experiment heterogeneity. Researchers cannot tell which precision regime they are in, and high- and low-replicability sequences cannot be distinguished in principle. Binary verdicts systematically understate uncertainty in reporting scientific results, making high- and low-replicability sequences appear discriminable, when they are not. Common sources of heterogeneity amplify these problems as shown in a reanalysis of Many Labs~4 data. The practice of aggregating replicability rates across heterogeneous literatures produces averages that conflate incommensurable experimental regimes and lack a stable scientific interpretation. Hence, replicability rates as currently used do not provide a reliable demarcation criterion for scientific results. The replication crisis, if there is one, cannot be established by methods used to declare it.
