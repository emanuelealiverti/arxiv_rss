Overlap, Not Moments: Finite-Sample Theory for Bridge Sampling

medium Giorgio Micaletto · stat.CO, stat.ME, stat.TH · 14 Oct 2026
Develops finite-sample theory for bridge sampling to estimate marginal likelihoods.

Abstract

Bridge sampling estimates of marginal likelihoods are routinely reported with a delta-method Monte Carlo standard error (MCSE). Existing theory is asymptotic and cannot say whether a given run is long enough to trust the estimate or its MCSE. We give finite-sample answers. The optimal bridge estimator fits a logistic regression that separates posterior from proposal draws, and its accuracy is governed by their overlap, within a factor of two of the classification’s Bayes error. Because the estimating function is a sum of bounded terms, no moment conditions are needed, and all bounds hold uniformly over targets, proposals and dimensions. A number of draws inversely proportional to the overlap suffices and is necessary in the worst case, paralleling the exponential-in-Kullback–Leibler requirement of importance sampling. A Berry–Esseen bound with an explicit constant holds at the optimal rate, and the MCSE is accurate once the effective overlap is large; it is estimated at no cost. For Markov chain draws, the required sample size grows in proportion to the sampler’s relaxation time, unavoidably in the worst case. The estimated bridge, the fixed-point iteration and the fitted proposal matter only at second order, and we give free diagnostics for sampler failure and misestimated autocorrelation.
