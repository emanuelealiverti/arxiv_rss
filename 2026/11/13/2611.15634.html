Sampling the Funnel: A Comprehensive Survey of Hamiltonian Monte Carlo for Hierarchical Bayesian Inference

high Miguel Fernandez-de-Retana, Aitor Almeida · stat.CO, stat.ME, stat.ML · 13 Nov 2026
The paper surveys Hamiltonian Monte Carlo variants and their application to hierarchical Bayesian inference.

Abstract

Hierarchical models are central to applied Bayesian statistics, yet the variance structures they depend on can produce posterior geometries that remain difficult to explore even with Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (HMC), a designation now covering a large and increasingly heterogeneous family of algorithms. This survey uses Generalized HMC as an organizing framework to describe the many variants of HMC, their methodological and computational properties, and their empirical consequences. Varying the trajectory length, the degree of momentum refreshment, and the presence of a Metropolis correction recovers several familiar Hamiltonian and Langevin samplers as special cases. Three further, largely independent directions account for much of the remaining literature: modifying the metric, as in Riemannian HMC; modifying the Hamiltonian used by the acceptance test, as in modified or shadow HMC; and modifying the numerical integrator, as in multi-stage splitting schemes. We review these variants alongside the procedures used to tune and diagnose them, with particular attention to the effective sample size, whose standard estimators assume a reversibility that generalized and modified samplers lack, and ignore the importance weights that modified Hamiltonians introduce. We close with the open problems these methods face for hierarchical models, taking joint longitudinal and time-to-event models as the motivating example.
