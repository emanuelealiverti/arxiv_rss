Estimation and Inference for Multiplicative Error Models via Analytical Derivatives

medium Luca Scaffidi Domianello · stat.CO · 13 Nov 2026
Derives analytical score and Hessian for multiplicative error model log-likelihood and provides robust covariance estimators.

Abstract

Maximum Likelihood Estimates (MLEs) of the parameters of a Multiplicative Error Model are typically computed through a standard gradient method, an iterative procedure usually based on numerical approximations to derivatives of the log-likelihood function, owing to the complexity of the analytical derivatives, which involve recursive terms. Building on previous work developed mainly in the GARCH framework, we derive analytical expressions for the score vector and the Hessian matrix of the Multiplicative Error Model log-likelihood by adopting the Gamma distribution for estimation purposes. Then, we illustrate how the iterative algorithm based on the analytical derivatives should be computationally implemented. After maximizing the log-likelihood, we also provide explicit formulas for several estimators of the asymptotic covariance matrix of the MLE. Additionally, we provide analytic formulas for robust covariance estimators that remain valid under distributional misspecification, which are also referred to as the asymptotic variance of the Quasi-Maximum Likelihood Estimator. We also propose a covariance estimator that is consistent under general forms of heteroskedasticity of the error term, while the usually adopted estimators rely on a specific variance assumption. The finite-sample performance of the covariance estimators is assessed through a Monte Carlo investigation. An application on real data completes the analysis.
