Inference with weights: Residualization produces short, valid intervals for varying estimands and varying resampling processes

low Erin Hartman, Chad Hazlett, Arisa Sadeghpour · stat.ME · 13 Nov 2026
Provides residualized weighted regression standard errors that yield shorter, valid confidence intervals across estimands and resampling methods.

Abstract

Weighting is widely used in observational causal inference, yet basic questions remain about how to estimate the variance of weighted effect estimates. For example, when and how should sampling uncertainty in the weights be propagated into the final result? Should these weights reduce rather than inflate variance? Does the choice of weighted variance estimator depend on the target estimand and the assumed source of randomness? We show that a single, familiar estimator addresses these concerns across settings: robust standard errors from a weighted regression of the outcome on treatment, the centered balancing covariates, and their interaction with treatment. These residualized standard errors are valid and often substantially smaller than those from weighted regression on treatment alone, with reductions of up to 30% when covariates are prognostic. We establish their justification under design-based, model-based, and superpopulation frameworks, for both ATE and ATT estimands, and for exact balancing, approximate balancing, and inverse propensity weights. For inference to the superpopulation, we employ a simple correction. We further show that M-estimation yields nearly identical results when constructed to answer the same questions, and that the key issue is not “uncertainty in the weights” given a sample, but uncertainty over the sample. Simulations and three empirical applications confirm our analyses.
