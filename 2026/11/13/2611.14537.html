Modeling Drug Overdose Mortality Levels Using Social Vulnerability Indicators in U.S. Counties: A Double Machine Learning Approach for Causal Analysis

low Qunhui Guo, Jiongbai Liu, Md Noor E Alam · stat.AP · 13 Nov 2026
Applies double machine learning to assess nonlinear effects of social vulnerability on drug overdose mortality.

Abstract

Drug poisoning mortality (DPM) remains a leading cause of death among working-age Americans, and its burden falls unevenly across communities. Existing county-level studies of social vulnerability and overdose have largely reported predictive or associational relationships, while the shape of the adjusted exposure-response relationship between overall social vulnerability and mortality remains unclear. We examined the CDC/ATSDR Social Vulnerability Index (SVI) and county drug poisoning mortality across three two-year windows spanning 2016-2021. The associational analysis compared linear regression, Random Forest, and XGBoost models of log mortality using period-specific SVI subfeatures and county covariates. The selected XGBoost models were interpreted with SHAP values. The double machine learning (DML) analysis estimated the exposure-response relationship between SVI and DPM and tested the linearity restriction. Socioeconomic vulnerability contributed positively to predicted mortality, with disability and unemployment among the strongest subfeatures, while racial and ethnic minority composition contributed negatively in all three periods. Summing the four theme contributions reduced the overall-index attribution by 34% to 36%, indicating substantial offsetting. Linearity was rejected in every window ($p < 10^{-26}$). The adjusted relationship increased through the middle of the SVI distribution and declined in the upper tail. This pattern remained stable both before and after the outbreak of the epidemic. Overall SVI therefore captured meaningful variation in overdose mortality but did not function as a uniformly increasing risk scale across its full range. Component-level interpretation and current overdose surveillance provide important context for interpreting the composite index.
