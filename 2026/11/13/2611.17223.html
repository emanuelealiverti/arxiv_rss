Combinatorial Bernoulli Factories with Polynomially Many Coins

medium Renato Paes Leme, Rad Niazadeh · stat.CO · 13 Nov 2026
Presents polynomial‑time algorithms to generate polytope vertices from coin flips.

Abstract

We provide an efficient implementation of combinatorial Bernoulli factories. Such a factory turns coins of unknown biases $x$ into a random vertex of a polytope $P$ whose mean is exactly $x$. For every affine slice of the cube (the class of polytopes that admit a factory), we give a single algorithm whose expected number of coin flips and running time at every interior $x$ are polynomial in the dimension and in $1/{\delta_}$, where ${\delta_}=\min_i{x_i,1-x_i}$ is never supplied to the algorithm. For perfect matchings this gives $O(n^{8}{\delta_}^{-2}\log(n/{\delta_}))$ flips, while the factory of Niazadeh, Paes Leme, and Schneider [STOC 2021] spends at least $n^{2n-1}$ flips in expectation at the uniform doubly stochastic matrix. For other polytopes, our method rounds exactly given a point deep inside $P$ and the promise that $x$ is far from the boundary of $P$.
