Can We Ignore Data Dependence in Inference Implementation? A Positive Answer for Regression Discontinuity Designs

low Zihao Yuan · stat.ME, stat.TH · 13 Nov 2026
The paper proposes a validity recovery procedure for regression discontinuity inference that maintains asymptotic coverage despite data dependence.

Abstract

In empirical economics, regression discontinuity design (RDD) inference is commonly based on asymptotic normality under an independence assumption, but the running variables or disturbances may exhibit unknown and complex dependence. We first rigorously identify a fundamental dilemma for this conventional practice. To address it, we propose a validity recovery procedure that redefines the roles of bias and stochastic calibration, thereby decoupling data dependence from stochastic calibration. Taking the independence-assumption-based confidence interval as our inferential anchor, our procedure restores its validity while preserving its asymptotic leading constant, even when the data are dependent. For fixed-design RDD based on local linear regression, our intervals achieve asymptotically exact nominal coverage and, compared with the anchor, exploit higher-order smoothness without enlarging the asymptotic variance constant, attaining the minimax-optimal shrinking rates up to a mild factor, such as $\sqrt{\log T})$. The procedure requires neither specific dependence frameworks (e.g., mixing or physical dependence) nor dependence-modeling techniques (e.g., long-run variance estimation). For random-design RDD, under more specific dependence assumptions, we derive the rate-explicit coverage error at each nominal level and characterize the trade-off between this rate and interval length. Moreover, modeling dependence becomes an optional enhancement that can further enable validity recovery and improve finite-sample coverage, while averaging over repeated calibrations stabilizes the randomized center and protects against undercoverage in finite samples without increasing interval length. In short, under mild dependence and moment conditions, our procedure makes data independence an implementation default rather than a validity requirement. Simulations support the theoretical results.
