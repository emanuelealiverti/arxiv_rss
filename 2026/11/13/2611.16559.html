Inverse Confounding Analysis: Part 2. Applications to Real- World Observational Data

low Sergey Porotsky · stat.ME · 13 Nov 2026
Proposes exact inverse confounding analysis for quantifying significance of unmeasured confounding via risk ratio range.

Abstract

Unmeasured confounding factors in observational data may lead to biased estimates of the effect of an exposure on an outcome. Consequently, a central problem in causal inference based on observational data is sensitivity analysis with respect to unmeasured confounding. Existing sensitivity-analysis methods generally focus on worst-case bounds. We propose an exact method for quantifying the significance of confounding, defined here in terms of the complete range of analytical values of the stratification-based Risk Ratio over the set of all joint distributions compatible with the observed characteristics. We refer to the proposed method as Inverse Confounding Analysis (ICA) - it is described in [1]. This paper presents examples of applying ICA to several real-world statistical datasets and examines an important issue that has attracted criticism of the method: the practical feasibility of obtaining the input data required for its application. The results are compared with those of two other methods for confounding analysis, the E-value and the method proposed by Pena.
