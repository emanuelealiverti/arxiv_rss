Conformal prediction for censored life history data

medium Lu Mao · stat.ME, stat.ML · 13 Nov 2026
Develops conformal prediction methods for calibrated prediction intervals on censored multistate life‑history summaries.

Abstract

We consider individual prediction of general summaries of a multistate trajectory, such as time alive out of hospital or time spent in remission. Right censoring complicates calibration because these outcomes may be only partially observed. We develop a conformal prediction approach for scalar and vector summaries over a fixed clinical horizon supported by follow-up. For each candidate region, we define the coverage determining time as the earliest time at which the observed history establishes whether the outcome lies in the region, even if the outcome itself is still unknown. We weight observed coverage decisions by the inverse probability of remaining uncensored to that time and augment the estimator with conditional coverage probabilities for unresolved histories. For a fixed region, the coverage estimator is doubly robust with respect to the censoring and conditional coverage models and attains the semiparametric efficiency bound when both are consistent at suitable rates. For the calibrated region, we derive a finite-sample lower bound on the coverage probability and establish asymptotic validity if either model is correct. In simulations, using partial histories yields narrower prediction intervals at comparable coverage. We illustrate joint prediction of days in and out of hospital in the HF-ACTION trial and prediction of relapse-free time in the German Breast Cancer Study Group cohort.
