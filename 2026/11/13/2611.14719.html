Martingale posteriors for flexible regression via recursive least squares

high Davide Agnoletto, Tommaso Rigon, David B. Dunson · stat.ME · 13 Nov 2026
Introduces a predictive Bayes recursive least squares framework yielding a closed‑form martingale posterior for flexible regression.

Abstract

Predictive Bayes quantifies uncertainty by specifying a sequence of one-step-ahead predictive distributions, inducing a martingale posterior without an explicit prior. Existing parametric constructions rely on stochastic-gradient updates and have seen limited development for flexible regression. We propose a predictive Bayes framework based on recursive least squares, whose martingale posterior has an explicit representation: given future covariates, it is the law of the posterior mean plus a weighted series of independent draws from the standardized predictive distribution. The posterior mean is a penalized least squares estimator, available in closed form and invariant to data ordering, whereas the predictive distribution, for example heavy tailed, is chosen separately without changing the estimator or algorithm. We derive the exact posterior variance under a known design and an asymptotically Gaussian approximation, and obtain the martingale posterior in closed form for Gaussian predictive distributions. Posterior draws are independent by construction, with a known truncation error, and require no Markov chain Monte Carlo. The construction extends to additive, mixed and Gaussian process models. In simulations and an analysis of NHANES triglyceride data, the method attains accuracy comparable to that of a fully Bayesian model with the same response distribution at a fraction of its computational cost.
