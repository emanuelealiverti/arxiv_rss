Calibrated Order-Randomized Rosenblatt Tests

medium Mehrdad Pournaderi · stat.ME, stat.AP · 9 Nov 2026
The paper introduces order‑randomized Rosenblatt tests that calibrate power for multivariate distribution conformity testing.

Abstract

We test whether a multivariate vector X conforms to a specified distribution F, as in copula modelling and density forecasting. The Rosenblatt transform reduces this to testing uniformity, but its arbitrary coordinate ordering affects power under dependence. We study order randomization: transforming under many random orderings and merging evidence with dependence-robust rules. Reordering only redistributes the Mahalanobis signal energy, so one ordering is a lucky or unlucky draw, and pooling raises calibrated power. We compare against two order-invariant baselines, the chi-squared energy test and the symmetric-root test. With F known, the e-value average over orderings is at least as powerful as the symmetric-root test in every setting we ran, and as the chi-squared test on concentrated and fully diffuse departures. With Gaussian mean and covariance estimated, the classical conditional probability integral transform keeps each ordering’s p-values exact and e-values valid, a parametric bootstrap calibrates the pooled tests, and the comparisons hold except for deficits of at most 0.02 against the symmetric-root test on single-coordinate departures. Validating a Gaussian risk model for nine currencies, the test flags Brexit, COVID and other episodes; it extends to any null with computable, simulable conditionals.
