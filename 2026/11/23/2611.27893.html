Multi-scale dynamic latent space models improve inference for a social predator

high Xinyi Lu, T. J. Clark-Wolf, Matthew C. Metz · stat.AP · 23 Nov 2026
Extends dynamic latent space network models with coupled two-scale autoregressive processes to capture within- and between-group dynamics.

Abstract

Social networks are dynamic in nature. Examining changes in dynamic networks provides insights into the drivers and consequences of social interactions. Dynamic latent space models (DLSMs) quantify social tie strength through a distance measure between latent positions in an unobservable, low-dimensional space. Conditional on the latent positions, ties are assumed independent, and temporal dynamics are introduced through autocorrelated trajectories. These trajectories are typically modeled as random walks; however, random walks lack the mechanism to characterize network clustering, where individuals from the same group tend to form stronger ties than those across groups. Therefore, we extend the framework of DLSM to distinguish within-group dynamics from between-group dynamics. Our approach employs coupled movement models operating at two scales in the latent space to encode community structure using autoregressive processes with attraction. Through simulation studies, we show that when cluster separation is high, our framework improves coverage of linear predictors and enhances overall model fit relative to a naive DLSM. When cluster separation is moderate, performance remains comparable to that of the naive approach. In addition, our method demonstrates empirical computational advantages over the naive DLSM, with potential for further efficiency gains. We apply our framework to a case study of gray wolf (Canis lupus) interactions over three decades in Yellowstone National Park, where it provides improved model performance and novel insights into wolf social dynamics.
