Uncovering Multimorbidity Patterns Through a Novel Randomized Clustering Algorithm

low Ali Jafari, Hari Balasubramanian · stat.AP, stat.ME · 23 Nov 2026
Introduces a randomized mixed-integer clustering algorithm using lift thresholds to identify multimorbidity patterns in MEPS survey data.

Abstract

Patients with several chronic conditions rarely share the same combination: in four years of the nationally representative Medical Expenditure Panel Survey (MEPS), we find 32,789 distinct combinations of two to seven conditions, and over 90% of those involving four or more occur in a single patient. Methods that search for common combinations discard most of this heterogeneity. We present the Randomized Length Sequential Clustering Algorithm (RLSCA), which links diseases by lift, a pairwise association measure counted only above a user-set support threshold. A mixed-integer program repeatedly selects and removes the group of conditions with the greatest internal lift, up to a random size; 100 iterations yield a database of clusters scored by size, rediscovery frequency, and body systems spanned. In MEPS 2019, stratified by age and sex, RLSCA recovers known groupings at a high threshold, such as diabetes with lipid disorders and hypertension, and at a lower threshold returns clusters of up to eight conditions spanning five body systems. Frequent itemset mining, at its lowest feasible support, finds no set above five conditions; hierarchical clustering cannot control cluster size. With a clinical large language model, the database could suggest which further conditions a patient is at risk of.
