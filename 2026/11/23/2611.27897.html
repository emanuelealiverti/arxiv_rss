Extreme Value Inference with Measurement Error: Application to Earthquake Magnitudes

medium Wanchen Yue, Israel Martinez Hernandez, Jonathan A. Tawn, Ross Towe, Zak Varty · stat.ME, stat.AP · 23 Nov 2026
Develops partially censored likelihood inference for noisy extremes using a hybrid nonparametric-body/GPD-tail model with covariate-dependent parameters.

Abstract

Extreme value analyses typically assume that observations are measured without error, yet many environmental measurements are subject to different levels of observational uncertainty depending on the context. Ignoring such measurement error can distort inference on the tail behaviour. In this paper, we focus on peaks-over-threshold models, where exceedances above a high threshold are typically modelled using the generalised Pareto distribution (GPD). We develop a statistical framework for extreme value inference when observations are noisy measurements of an underlying true variable. Measurement error introduces a particular challenge in threshold-based extreme value analysis, as latent exceedances may be observed below the threshold, while latent non-exceedances may be observed above it. To address this problem, we represent the latent distribution using a hybrid body–tail specification, combining a flexible nonparametric body with a GPD tail. We develop a computationally efficient partially censored likelihood inference approach for data of this structure. The framework allows the measurement error scale and the GPD parameters to depend on covariates. Simulation studies demonstrate biases on the tail inference from ignoring measurement error and that our approach overcomes such impacts. The methodology is motivated by, and illustrated using, the catalogue of induced earthquake magnitudes from the Groningen, Netherlands, one of the largest gas fields in the world.
