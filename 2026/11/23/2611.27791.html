Synthetic Surrogate Functional Regression (SSFR): A Robust Framework for Estimating Spatially Varying Covariate Effects in Surrogate-Augmented Neuroimaging

medium Guannan Wang, Yang Long, Shan Yu, Zhiling Gu, Lily Wang · stat.AP, stat.ME · 23 Nov 2026
Proposes a robust two-stage surrogate-augmented functional regression framework with asymptotic theory, surrogate screening, and bootstrap simultaneous confidence corridors for neuroimaging.

Abstract

Relating demographic, clinical, and genetic covariates to high-dimensional imaging outcomes is a core problem in brain imaging, especially when the target modality is observed for only a subset of participants. Generative models can synthesize missing modalities from auxiliary data, but using synthetic images in regression requires care to ensure valid inference. We propose a two-stage Synthetic Surrogate Functional Regression (SSFR) framework for spatially varying covariate effects under partially observed imaging outcomes. By calibrating surrogate information against observed target images, SSFR improves efficiency while remaining robust to discrepancies between the surrogate and target regression functions. We establish consistency and the convergence rate of the estimator, and show that first-stage surrogate-model estimation error does not alter its asymptotic rate. We introduce a voxelwise surrogate explanatory measure $R^2(z)$, the fraction of residual target-image variation explained by a candidate surrogate; with the observation rate, it gives a principled criterion for surrogate screening and indicates the potential efficiency gain. We further develop a weighted bootstrap procedure for simultaneous confidence corridors (SCCs). As surrogate informativeness increases, SCC widths approach those obtained under complete observation, improving localization of significant effects that cannot be identified from the observed data alone. Simulations confirm accuracy and robustness under surrogate misspecification and with surrogates generated by image-to-image neural networks. Applied to spatially normalized PET images from ADNI, SSFR yields interpretable coefficient maps and uniformly narrower SCCs than an analysis using observed PET alone, enlarging the region over which age-related metabolic decline is simultaneously significant.
