Trajectory-based cycle-level pedestrian crash prediction at intersections: A novel non-linear link function for block maxima led Bayesian GEV framework addressing heterogeneous traffic conditions

medium Parvez Anowar, Nazmul Haque, Md Asif Raihan, Md Hadiuzzaman · stat.AP, stat.ME · 23 Nov 2026
Introduces a nonlinear-link hierarchical Bayesian GEV model, fitted by MCMC, for cycle-level pedestrian crash risk at signalized intersections.

Abstract

This study develops a cycle-level framework for estimating pedestrian crash risk at signalized intersections under heterogeneous, non-lane-based traffic. Existing approaches often assume linear relationships between covariates and parameters, oversimplifying the complex, non-monotonic interactions among motorized vehicles (MVs), non-motorized vehicles (NMVs), and pedestrians. To address this limitation, the framework introduces a non-linear link function within a Bayesian generalized extreme value (GEV) structure to capture how traffic effects vary. It applies extreme value theory through the block maxima approach using post-encroachment time as a surrogate safety measure, with each signal cycle serving as an analysis block. It also proposes a behaviour-normalized modified crash risk (MRC) metric to account for pedestrians’ habitual risk-taking behaviour in Dhaka’s mixed traffic. Seven hierarchical Bayesian models were estimated using Markov Chain Monte Carlo simulation and compared using the deviance information criterion. Models with a non-linear link function only in the GEV location parameter fit the data better than their linear counterparts. Results suggest pedestrian speed is negatively associated with crash risk, while flow and speed of MVs, pedestrian flow, and NMV conflicting speed show positive associations, though all remain uncertain. The selected model’s MRC estimate was close to the observed crashes at a 93% confidence level tuned to this dataset. These results suggest that the non-linear structure adds flexibility for capturing the non-stationary and non-linear nature of traffic extremes. This framework is a step towards proactive identification of pedestrian crash risk and after wider validation, it could support adaptive signal control and safety interventions in mixed-traffic environments.
