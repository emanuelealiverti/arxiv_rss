Beware of complexity bias: A hindrance to conceptual model generalization and interpretability

low Joseph Janssen, Yang Yang · stat.AP · 23 Nov 2026

Abstract

In the field of rainfall-runoff modeling, lumped conceptual models have been shown to be powerful representations of catchment filtering functions. They demonstrate fairly strong predictive performance in gauged catchments and have the ability to learn important intermediate latent variables and flow paths which increases their value as interpretable models. During calibration, these conceptual models may fit streamflow just as well as deep learning models, however, their performance often sharply degrades after deployment, suggesting the presence of overfitting. We hypothesize that complexity (or flexibility) bias is one of the causes of such overfitting. Complexity bias can appear within conceptual models when the number of effective degrees of freedom is parameter or flow path dependent. Using a popular conceptual model and a series of synthetic datasets, we illustrate the presence of complexity bias and demonstrate its impact on model interpretability/parameter inference. Then, using real rainfall-runoff data from CAMELS-US, we investigate if overfitting can be reduced by adding regularization terms that prevent complexity bias from forming. Our results suggest that conceptual and hybrid modeling can be further improved if hydrologists become aware of this pervasive bias.
