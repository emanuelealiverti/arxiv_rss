Bessel-Debiased Pseudo-Marginal MCMC for Generalised Bayesian Inference

low Yingkai Lu, Jeong Eun Lee, Geoff K. Nicholls · stat.CO, stat.ME · 23 Nov 2026

Abstract

Generalised Bayesian inference exponentiates a weighted loss, sometimes an integral estimated by simulation. Loss estimators generally give biased posterior weights which Pseudo-Marginal MCMC does not correct, and greater variability raises computational cost. We introduce Sign-Corrected Bessel Debiasing (SCBD), a signed pseudo-marginal method using independent block estimates from ordinary Monte Carlo or randomised quasi-Monte Carlo (RQMC). The correction is exact for i.i.d. Gaussian block estimates with unknown variance. For non-Gaussian blocks, we bound the posterior error in total variation. Under regularity conditions, the correction improves the fixed-data Monte Carlo bound from $O(M_n^{-1})$ to $O(M_n^{-2})$, where $M_n$ counts loss-integrand evaluations per loss estimate. Suppose the RQMC variance for a block of size $B$ is uniformly $O(B^{-\alpha}(\log B)^{d-1})$ in fixed dimension $d$. With a fixed number of blocks and a learning rate proportional to the sample size $n$, a per-proposal budget of order $n^{2/\alpha}$, up to logarithmic factors, suffices to keep the leading log-weight variance bounded. Our examples show that favourable RQMC representations can inherit this budget scaling and that RQMC variance reduction complements Bessel correction. Once the block estimates have been formed, the Bessel correction comes at negligible additional cost. The correction is generic, straightforward to implement, and supported by theory.
