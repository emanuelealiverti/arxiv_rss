Deep conditional distribution estimation via nonparametric exponential tilt models

medium Junjun Lang, Yukun Liu, Jing Qin · stat.ME · 23 Nov 2026
Proposes nonparametric exponential tilt models for conditional density estimation, with empirical-likelihood inference, convergence guarantees, and independence testing.

Abstract

Estimating the conditional distribution of a response $Y$ given covariates $X$ is a fundamental problem in statistics, underlying prediction, feature estimation, and dependence assessment. We propose a unified nonparametric representation of the conditional density, a nonparametric exponential tilt model, in which the dependence between $Y$ and $X$ is captured by an interaction function $\phi(x,y)$ while both baseline densities are left unspecified. We establish identifiability and develop a nonparametric likelihood method that profiles out the baselines by empirical likelihood, with non-asymptotic error bounds under general sieve conditions. Because the profile likelihood admits no closed-form or readily tractable population counterpart, standard empirical process arguments do not apply; we therefore establish a new exponential tail bound for the supremum of a canonical U-process under a uniform entropy condition, a result we show to be new to the U-process literature and of independent interest, with a largely self-contained proof. The framework unifies several inferential tasks: mutual density ratio and mutual information estimation; a general theory for conditional features, including means, quantiles and distribution functions, without separate smoothness assumptions on the individual conditional feature functions; and a finite-sample valid permutation test of independence. Under deep neural network sieves, the convergence rates depend on the intrinsic complexity of $\phi$ rather than the ambient dimension. Simulations demonstrate that the proposed method attains uniformly smaller estimation errors than existing conditional density and mutual density-ratio estimators, and delivers markedly higher power in independence testing when dependence is oscillatory or high-dimensional, while remaining comparable to established tests in standard settings.
