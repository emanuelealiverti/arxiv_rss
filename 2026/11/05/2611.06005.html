Time-Varying Tails: Estimation, Inference, and Risk Measurement

medium Shuo Li, Liuhua Peng · stat.ME · 5 Nov 2026
Estimates time-varying extreme value index using a ratio‑form kernel Hill estimator and constructs confidence bands for risk measures.

Abstract

Motivated by evidence that the tails of economic and financial time series need not be stable over time, we develop a framework for studying time-varying tails. We model the extreme value index as a smooth function of time in the locally stationary framework of Zhou and Wu (2009). We estimate it by a ratio-form kernel Hill estimator in which a single intermediate quantile thresholds the whole local window. We establish consistency and asymptotic normality, and we construct uniform confi- dence bands. We also propose a consistent test of tail stability under extremal clustering. Combined with the Weissman extrapolation method, the estimator of the extreme value index yields time- varying extreme Value-at-Risk and Expected Shortfall. In an empirical application, we find the tail of daily loss of S&P 500 is compatible with a constant shape, whereas that of volatility-filtered loss varies significantly.
