Spatiotemporal Causal Inference with Spillover and Carryover Effects: Airstrikes and Insurgent Violence in Vietnam and Afghanistan

medium Mitsuru Mukaigawara, Kosuke Imai, Jason Lyall, Georgia Papadogeorgou · stat.ME, stat.AP · 25 Nov 2026
Develops a spatiotemporal causal inference framework preserving treatment, mediator, and outcome locations and timing, applied to airstrikes and insurgent violence.

Abstract

Do airstrikes suppress insurgent violence or provoke it? Answering such questions requires methods that preserve the spatial and temporal structure of airstrikes and insurgent retaliation. Yet nearly all microlevel research aggregates fine-grained data into panels built on large administrative units and long time intervals. This practice forecloses direct tests of causal mechanisms and imposes restrictive assumptions about spillover and carryover that bias estimates. We develop a practical framework for spatiotemporal causal inference in which treatment, mediator, and outcome all retain their precise locations and timing. Applying it to declassified airstrike data from Vietnam and Afghanistan, we find that bombing displaces, but does not suppress, insurgent violence. Insurgent retaliation follows predictable patterns, concentrating near rebel-controlled hamlets (Vietnam) and U.S. bases (Afghanistan) and emerging about a week later. Civilian harm does not mediate this response. Post-airstrike insurgent violence appears less about avenging aggrieved civilians than demonstrating resolve by imposing costs on counterinsurgents.
