Generalizing Couple-Randomized Trials Under Limited Overlap and Missing Partner Linkage

medium Subir Hait · stat.ME, stat.AP · 25 Nov 2026
Derives identification conditions and sharp bounds for generalizing couple-randomized trials, estimating effects via Bayesian dyadic multilevel regression and poststratification.

Abstract

We study how to generalize couple-randomized trials when some couple types are absent from the trial and the target survey reports partner characteristics without linking spouses. Under an additive effect model with unrestricted partner-profile terms, the target average effect is identified exactly when the wife and husband margins assign equal mass to each connected component of the trial support graph. A specified interaction is identified when its alternating contrast around at least one cycle is nonzero. Without linkage, we obtain sharp bounds by optimizing over pairings consistent with the survey margins. A spousal odds ratio can narrow the bounds for a binary interaction. We derive joint bounds for an index-patient design, quantify bias from common shortcuts, and estimate effects and bounds using Bayesian dyadic multilevel regression and poststratification. In a worked example based on 3,826 Health and Retirement Study couples and simulated trial outcomes, an index-patient trial and an unlinked survey yielded a 2.93 mmHg wide identified set for the couple-average effect. Linking the survey narrowed it to 2.38 mmHg. Adding 300 couples without diabetes narrowed the unlinked set to 0.28 mmHg and yielded point identification with linkage under the fitted model.
