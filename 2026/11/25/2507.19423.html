Perfect Clustering in Extremely Sparse Multilayer Networks with Groupwise Subspace Structure

high Marianna Pensky · stat.ML, stat.ME, stat.TH · 25 Nov 2026
Tensor-based clustering of layers in extremely sparse multilayer random dot product graphs achieves perfect recovery of groupwise latent subspace structure.

Abstract

The paper studies a multilayer network on the same collection of nodes, where each layer follows the Generalized Random Dot Product Graph Model. Furthermore, all layers can be partitioned into groups, each group with its own unique latent subspace structure, but otherwise the matrices of connection probabilities can all be different. This setting is extremely general and includes most multilayer network models as special cases. In addition, we are interested in the situation where the layer networks may be extremely sparse and, therefore, the layer-by-layer analysis is unreliable. This problem can also be seen as a generalization of the classic setting in which one recovers a shared structure across an entire collection of matrices, extending it to the case where such structures are shared only within specific groups of matrices. The key task in this setting is to recover the groups of layers with unique subspace structures, since, after that, the information in those groups of layers can be pooled together by well-studied methods. Until now, clustering of layers in such networks was based on layer-by-layer analysis, which required the multilayer network to be sufficiently dense. Our paper is the first to provide a tensor-based approach to the problem that combines information from all layers in an unconventional manner. Our theoretical results, established under intuitive and non-restrictive assumptions, assert that the new technique achieves perfect clustering under sparsity conditions that, up to logarithmic factors, coincide with the computational lower bound derived for a much simpler model. The paper also delivers a nuanced message about the applicability of the new technique.
