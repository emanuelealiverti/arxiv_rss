Differential Item Functioning: Methodological Advances in Detection, Identification, and Interpretation

medium Gabriel Wallin, Qi Huang · stat.ME, stat.AP · 25 Nov 2026
A review of methods for detecting, identifying, and interpreting differential item functioning, comparing anchor-based, approximate-invariance, and latent-class approaches.

Abstract

Differential item functioning (DIF) occurs when an item’s response distribution differs across groups for respondents at the same level of the construct. Separating DIF from group differences in the construct itself requires identifying assumptions. Conventional analyses compare observed groups on a common scale established through items assumed to be invariant. We distinguish four settings according to whether the groups are observed or latent and whether the common scale is fixed by prespecified restrictions or estimated from the data. For observed groups, we review observed-score and item response theory procedures, multiple-covariate and intersectional models, and recursive partitioning. When anchors are unknown, we compare empirical anchor selection, partial and approximate invariance, robust scaling, regularisation and sparsity-based identification by the assumptions each makes about the pattern of DIF. For latent groups, we discuss mixture models and the joint estimation of class membership and DIF. Further topics include ordinal and multidimensional models, inference after data-dependent selection, effect sizes, consequences for composite scores, and the sources of DIF. We conclude with open problems, including anchor-free identification when DIF is widespread and inference when latent classes and invariant items are selected jointly.
