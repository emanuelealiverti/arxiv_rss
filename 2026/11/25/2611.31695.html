Dynamic Emulation for Bayesian Inverse Problems in Spatiotemporal Mechanistic Models

high Xiang Chen, Sudipto Banerjee · stat.CO · 25 Nov 2026
A matrix-variate dynamic linear model emulator enables efficient Hamiltonian Monte Carlo calibration of spatiotemporal mechanistic models without repeated simulator runs.

Abstract

Calibrating a spatiotemporal mechanistic model to field data is a Bayesian inverse problem in which every likelihood evaluation needs a simulator run. The R package spDBL replaces the simulator with a dynamic emulator, a matrix-variate dynamic linear model with a closed-form posterior that evolves the field in discrete time and processes large grids in spatial blocks. It then calibrates the parameters without further simulator runs, using Hamiltonian Monte Carlo (implemented in the Stan programming environment) conditionally on emulator draws. In benchmarks on epidemic fields, with every method tuned alike, spDBL’s predictions at untried inputs were the only ones with near-nominal interval coverage, though a tuned RobustGaSP was more accurate, and spDBL’s calibrations matched the accuracy of RobustCalibration with higher, though below nominal, interval coverage (80% against 67-76%) and over 50 times more effective samples per CPU second.
