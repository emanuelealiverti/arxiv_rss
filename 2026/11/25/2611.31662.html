Correcting for Differential Nonresponse Using Past Election Data

medium David Metrick, Harper Wang · stat.ME · 25 Nov 2026
Corrects differential survey nonresponse in U.S. election MRP estimates by transferring a log-odds correction estimated from the prior election.

Abstract

In an election survey, the probability of responding can depend on the vote being measured. When supporters of one candidate are less likely to respond, they are underrepresented in the sample and the estimated vote share is biased. Weighting and multilevel regression and poststratification (MRP) adjust for observed covariates but do not eliminate this bias when response rates differ by vote within demographic groups. We ask whether this differential nonresponse can be corrected using the previous election, whose outcome is known. We estimate a two-parameter correction on the log-odds scale against one election’s results, apply it unchanged to the next, and compare the corrected estimates with its results. The application is the Cooperative Election Study for the 2020 and 2024 U.S. presidential elections, with the 436 congressional districts as units. Among validated voters in 2020, Republicans appear in the sample at about 0.6 times the odds of Democrats, and in both elections district-level MRP understates the Republican two-party share by 16 to 18 percentage points on average. A correction estimated on 2020 and applied to 2024 gives 95% posterior predictive bands containing the actual result in 98.2% of districts, compared with 91.5% for a correction on the share scale. About two-thirds of the curvature in the 2024 error was already present in 2020, and the transferred correction removes about 69% of it; the remaining third, a level shift of 1.7 points, and a few districts (several with very large 2020-to-2024 swings) are specific to 2024. We also fit a model for variation in differential nonresponse across districts (Little and Gelman, 1998) and find that the design weights account for about 62% of the selection on the log-odds scale. Two variants that apply the correction within poststratification cells, specified in advance, did not improve on the simpler correction.
