Breaking Resonance: Square-root acceleration for Metropolis-adjusted randomized Hamiltonian Monte Carlo

high Nawaf Bou-Rabee · stat.CO · 25 Nov 2026
Proves square-root condition-number mixing bounds for randomized Hamiltonian Monte Carlo beyond Gaussian targets using non-resonant integration-time laws.

Abstract

Hamiltonian Monte Carlo with a fixed integration time can fail to mix because of resonances, and randomizing the integration time removes them. We prove that Metropolis-adjusted HMC with velocity Verlet and random integration times mixes from a warm start in a number of transitions that does not depend on the condition number, for every integration-time law satisfying a Fourier condition that we call non-resonant; uniform, exponential and triangular laws are examples. For a target density proportional to $e^{-U}$ on $\mathbb R^d$, where $U$ is $m$-strongly convex with $L$-Lipschitz gradient and $L_H$-Lipschitz Hessian and $\kappa=L/m$, the number of gradient evaluations needed to reach a fixed total variation accuracy is, up to constants and logarithmic factors, $\sqrt{\kappa d}+\sqrt{L_H}\,(d/m)^{3/4}$. This is the first square-root condition-number bound for this sampler beyond Gaussian targets. The proof converts relative-entropy contraction of the unadjusted proposal into a conductance bound for the adjusted chain, and shows that the integration-time law enters only through its characteristic function. Hessian regularity cannot be dropped for a fixed step size: without it there are targets in the class for which the cost is at least of order $\kappa^{2/3}$, up to logarithmic factors, even from a warm start.
