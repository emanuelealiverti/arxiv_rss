Bayesian Learning of Spatially Heterogeneous Causal Structure in Spatial Transcriptomics

high Kyungwook Back, Junsouk Choi · stat.ME, stat.AP · 25 Nov 2026
SpaDAG learns spatially varying causal DAGs via Bayesian inference, with identifiability theory and an MCMC sampler for edge reversals across regions.

Abstract

Spatial transcriptomics preserves the location of each expression profile, making it possible to study how gene regulatory networks differ within a tissue. Across regions of a tissue, regulatory relationships may vary and even reverse direction, so identifying the causal relationships in a specific pathological region can provide insight into therapeutic targets. We propose SpaDAG, a Bayesian method for learning spatially varying causal directed acyclic graphs. By defining the graphs on a triangulation of the tissue, SpaDAG allows each edge to have its own spatial pattern and even to reverse direction across regions. We show that the graphs nonetheless remain fully identifiable, and develop an MCMC sampler that explores them efficiently by reversing an edge over an entire connected region in a single move. SpaDAG outperforms competing methods in simulations with and without direction reversals. In a section spanning a renal cell carcinoma and the adjacent kidney, SpaDAG finds largely different relationships in the two tissues, with those in the tumor concentrated on genes associated with therapeutic targets. Between two of these genes, it identifies a relationship whose direction reverses across the tumor boundary, generating hypotheses about their region-specific coupling.
