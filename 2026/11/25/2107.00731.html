Visualizing the geometry of labeled high-dimensional data with spheres

low Andrew D Zaharia, Anish S Potnis, Alexander Walther, Nikolaus Kriegeskorte · stat.CO · 25 Nov 2026
Fits hyperspheres to labeled high-dimensional data groups and visualizes them as spheres or circles in lower dimensions.

Abstract

Data visualizations can summarize high-dimensional distributions in two or three dimensions. Dimensionality reduction entails a loss of information, and what is preserved differs between methods. Existing methods preserve the local or the global geometry of the points, and most techniques do not consider labels. Here we introduce “hypersphere2sphere” (H2S), a new method that aims to visualize not the points, but the relationships between the labeled distributions. H2S fits a hypersphere to each labeled set of points in the high-dimensional space of the original data and visualizes each hypersphere as a sphere in 3D (or a circle in 2D). H2S perfectly captures the geometry of up to 4 high-dimensional hyperspheres using spheres in 3D (or 3 using circles in 2D), and approximates the geometry for larger numbers of distributions, matching the sizes (radii), and the pairwise separations (between-center distances) and the overlaps (along the center-connection line). The resulting visualizations are robust to sampling imbalances. Leveraging labels and the sphere as the simplest geometrical primitive, H2S provides an important addition to the toolbox of visualization techniques.
