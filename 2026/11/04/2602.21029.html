On the non-uniformity of the 2026 FIFA World Cup draw

low László Csató, Martin Becker, Karel Devriesere, Dries Goossens · stat.AP · 4 Nov 2026
Quantifies draw non‑uniformity and implements a workable integer‑programming solution.

Abstract

The group stage of a sports tournament is often made more appealing by introducing additional constraints in the group draw that promote an attractive and balanced group composition. For example, the number of intra-regional group matches is minimised in several world championships. Under such constraints, the traditional draw procedure may become non-uniform, meaning that the feasible allocations of the teams into groups are not equally likely to occur. Our paper quantifies this non-uniformity of the 2026 FIFA World Cup draw for the official draw procedure, as well as for 47 reasonable alternatives implied by all permutations of the four pots and two group labelling policies. We show why simulating with a recursive backtracking algorithm is intractable, and propose a workable implementation using integer programming. The official draw mechanism is found to be optimal based on two of five non-uniformity measures, including the average distortion of the 771 non-zero assignment probabilities. Nonetheless, non-uniformity can be more than halved if the organiser aims to treat the best teams drawn from the first pot equally.
