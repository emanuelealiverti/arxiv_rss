Functional nestedness of soil nematode communities across Europe: integrating morphological counts and eDNA reads

low Giuseppe Nicolosi, Michael Bonkowski, Carlo Febbi, Christian Mulder · stat.AP · 26 Nov 2026
Compares taxon turnover and nestedness in European soil nematode communities across land uses using morphological counts and eDNA metabarcoding data.

Abstract

  1. Soil nematodes occupy multiple trophic positions and contribute to key processes in soil food webs. Their communities vary across land uses and environmental conditions, yet it remains unclear whether these differences mainly reflect taxon replacement or nested patterns of taxon occurrence. 2. Here, we analysed two independent European datasets of soil nematode communities, SOB4ES, based on eDNA metabarcoding, and NemEu, based on morphological identification, to examine how genus composition varies across land uses. We partitioned community dissimilarity into turnover and nestedness-resultant components and compared sites under contrasting land uses with those sharing the same land use, while accounting for geographical and soil-related variation. We also assessed matrix-level nestedness, differences among trophic groups and associations between genus composition and environmental gradients. 3. In both datasets, genus turnover was consistently greater between contrasting land uses than within the same land use. Although the differences were modest, they remained significant after accounting for geographical distance and genus richness, whereas nestedness-resultant dissimilarity contributed little to land-use differentiation. These findings suggest that land-use-associated differences in European nematode communities are expressed primarily through changes in genus identity rather than nested patterns of taxon occurrence. 4. The consistency of this result across independently assembled datasets supports the broader relevance of taxonomic turnover for understanding soil biodiversity patterns across European land uses.
