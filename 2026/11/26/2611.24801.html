Bayesian Computation of Copula-based Multivariate Models with an Application to Doping Detection

high Emanuele Masillo, Pigi-Eva Vyltanioti, Nina Deliu, Brunero Liseo · stat.CO, stat.AP, stat.ME · 26 Nov 2026
Introduces a fully joint MCMC algorithm for copula-based multivariate Bayesian models, comparing it with a two-step IFM estimator under misspecification.

Abstract

The Athlete Biological Passport (ABP) monitors athletes for doping by comparing their biomarker readings against personalized reference ranges. These are estimated via the Bayesian ADAPTIVE model, which analyzes biomarkers separately and ignores their dependence. Building on a recently proposed copula-based multivariate extension of ADAPTIVE, this paper focuses on its computational aspects, introducing a fully joint MCMC algorithm and comparing it with a two-step Bayesian IFM estimator. Simulations show the two procedures perform comparably under a correctly specified copula, but exhibit a genuine trade-off under misspecification: the Bayesian IFM protects marginal inference, while the joint MCMC yields better-calibrated dependence estimates and is markedly faster. The two approaches are tested on a dataset containing ABP profiles of elite athletes, provided by NADO Italia, and collected as part of the project “Enhancing the methodological framework for doping detection: from univariate to multivariate testing analysis on Athlete Biological Passport profiles”. Both approaches achieve consistent classification performance, providing anti-doping organizations with two concrete inferential routes for adopting a multivariate ADAPTIVE model, along with guidance on the trade-offs between them.
