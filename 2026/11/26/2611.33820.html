Shooting for Fit: Risk-Averse Stochastic Roster Construction from Bayesian Shot-Pattern Models

medium Yue Yu, Cong Zhang, David Crandall, Guanyu Hu · stat.AP · 26 Nov 2026
Combines Bayesian shot-pattern modeling with risk-averse stochastic mixed-integer optimization to construct NBA rosters under salary-cap constraints.

Abstract

Roster construction is a critical decision problem in professional basketball, requiring teams to balance player quality, player complementarity, and financial constraints under uncertainty. Existing roster optimization models primarily rely on individual player ratings and aggregate performance statistics, which provide limited information about how players’ offensive contributions vary with their teammates. Recent advances in player-tracking technology have enabled statistical analyses of spatial shooting patterns, but these insights have rarely informed roster construction. We bridge this gap by developing a data-driven, risk-averse two-stage stochastic optimization framework for NBA roster construction. We first develop a Bayesian modeling framework that combines player statistics and spatial shot-chart data to identify player roles and estimate how teammate composition influences shot selection, shooting frequency, and scoring efficiency. The resulting model provides uncertainty-quantified offensive values for hypothetical five-player lineups, including combinations that have never played together. We then formulate roster construction as a two-stage stochastic mixed-integer program that optimizes player acquisitions under NBA salary-cap regulations while allowing lineup playing-time allocations to adapt to uncertain player roles. We evaluate the framework using 240 team-offseason decisions over eight seasons and two additional held-out seasons. The results demonstrate that incorporating spatial shooting compatibility substantially changes optimal roster decisions and improves predicted offensive performance relative to approaches based on individual player values. We further find that flexibility in allocating playing time across lineups absorbs much of the uncertainty about player roles.
