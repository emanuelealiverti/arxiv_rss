Pseudo-IPD Reconstruction from Published Kaplan-Meier Curves under Unidentified Within-Interval Censoring

medium Yi-Cheng Tai, Weijing Wang, Martin T. Wells · stat.ME · 26 Nov 2026
Characterizes non-identifiability of pseudo-IPD reconstruction from Kaplan-Meier curves and proposes systematic within-interval censoring allocation assessed via simulation.

Abstract

Reconstruction of pseudo-individual patient data (pseudo-IPD) from published Kaplan-Meier curves and number-at-risk tables is widely used to support secondary survival analyses when individual-level data are unavailable. However, the reconstruction is generally not unique because the published information does not determine how censoring is distributed within each risk-table interval. We study the non-identifiability that remains after interval-level event and censoring totals have been reconstructed. Different within-interval censoring placements can yield similarly good Kaplan-Meier agreement but different risk-set and censoring patterns, with possible effects on subsequent analyses. We characterize this non-identifiability, introduce a systematic allocation scheme, and assess its impact on reconstructed pseudo-IPD and downstream analyses. Simulation studies were conducted to examine how within-interval censoring allocation affects pseudo-IPD reconstruction under different risk-table resolutions and censoring settings. The proposed approach was also illustrated using published data from CheckMate 067. Our analysis suggests that considering multiple reconstructions provides a practical way to assess the impact of this non-identifiability on downstream analyses.
