Comparative Evaluation of Machine Learning Models for Predicting Donor Kidney Discard

low Peer Schliephacke, Hannah Schult, Leon Mizera, Judith Würfel, Gunter Grieser, Axel Rahmel, Carl-Ludwig Fischer-Fröhlich, Antje Jahn-Eimermacher · stat.AP · 26 Nov 2026
Benchmarks logistic regression, tree ensembles, boosting, and neural networks for predicting kidney discard, assessing discrimination, calibration, and feature importance.

Abstract

A kidney transplant can significantly improve the life expectancy and quality of life of patients with end-stage renal failure. Even more patients could be helped with a kidney transplant if the rate of kidneys that are discarded and not transplanted could be reduced. Machine learning (ML) techniques can support decision-making in this context by early identification of donor organs at high risk of discard. Although various ML models have been applied, their results are difficult to compare due to heterogenous datasets and differences in feature engineering and evaluation strategies. This study aims to provide a systematic and reproducible comparison of ML models for donor-level kidney discard prediction. We trained five ML models: Logistic Regression (LR), Decision Tree (DT), Random Forest (RF), Gradient Boosting (GB), and Multilayer Perceptrons (MLP) along with an ensemble model on data from 4,080 deceased donors in Germany. We implemented a benchmarking framework, including standardized feature engineering, supervised feature selection, and Bayesian hyperparameter optimization. Model performance was assessed for discrimination, calibration, and explainability. The ensemble achieved the highest discrimination performance. However, the results did not differ significantly from those of LR. Individual models such as LR, RF, and MLP performed comparably and substantially better than DT. Platt scaling generally improved calibration. SHAP identified age and renal markers as dominant predictors across models, reflecting clinical plausibility. This study demonstrates that consistent data preprocessing, feature selection, and evaluation can be more decisive for predictive success than the choice of the ML algorithm. The proposed benchmarking framework enables transparent comparison of models and provides a reproducible foundation for improving donor-level kidney discard prediction.
