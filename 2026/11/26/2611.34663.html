The Random Intercept Cross-Lagged Panel Model as a Statistical Model: Identification, Estimation, and Model Comparison

medium Zihao Cao · stat.ME · 26 Nov 2026
Derives identification conditions, likelihood scores, and population parameter relationships for random-intercept cross-lagged panel models.

Abstract

The random intercept cross-lagged panel model (RI-CLPM) distinguishes stable differences between people from lagged associations within individuals. We use a matrix representation to study its statistical properties for p variables measured across T waves. We derive a necessary and sufficient rank condition for global identification when transition matrices and innovation covariances vary by wave. Under time-invariant transitions, the condition specifies when three waves suffice. For complete Gaussian trajectories, matrix differentiation yields the profile likelihood score for each covariance and transition block. We also derive the population parameters of the conventional cross-lagged panel model (CLPM) when an RI-CLPM with fixed parameters generates the data. Our formulas quantify how CLPM slopes for observed variables differ from RI-CLPM slopes for within-person deviations at each transition. When the RI-CLPM transition matrix is time-invariant, the comparison also covers CLPM slopes pooled across waves. The formulas recover earlier bivariate results and show when cross-lagged slopes can appear, cancel, reverse sign, or change their ordering across models.
