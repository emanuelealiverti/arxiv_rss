Projection of multidimensional count data IRT models and their empirical indistinguishability

medium Loreen Sabel, Heinz Holling, Philipp Doebler · stat.ME · 26 Nov 2026
Extends projective IRT to multidimensional count data with Conway-Maxwell-Poisson models, deriving closed-form projected parameters and numerically approximating dispersion.

Abstract

Empirical test data is rarely unidimensional. If one dimension is of interest, projective item-response-theory (IRT) models can be used to work with a unidimensional model without neglecting the multidimensionality of the data. This work extends the projective IRT literature to count data IRT models from the Two-Parameter Conway-Maxwell-Poisson model framework. We build on the definition of empirical indistinguishability and analytically derive closed-form expressions for discrimination parameters and item intercepts after projection. The dispersion parameters of the projected Two- Parameter Conway-Maxwell-Poisson model are obtained with numerical approximations. The analytical calculations are not limited to the Conway-Maxwell-Poisson distribution. In addition, rotation methods are applied before projection. Results are illustrated and evaluated using empirical data from cognitive ability assessment items and in a simulation study. The projected model performed better in terms of RMSE, Spearman correlation between the true and estimated person parameters, and coverage of 95% confidence intervals based on the Fisher information than an approximation by a standard unidimensional model fitted to multidimensional data. No improvement in bias over the standard unidimensional model, nor improvement across all metrics over the multidimensional model, were observed.
