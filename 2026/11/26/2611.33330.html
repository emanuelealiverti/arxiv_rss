Horseshoe-Type Shrinkage from Ratio-Generated Priors

medium Radhika Kulkarni, Anirban DasGupta, Brani Vidakovic · stat.ME, stat.TH · 26 Nov 2026
Develops ratio-generated horseshoe-type shrinkage priors with origin and tail asymptotics, posterior behavior analysis, and wavelet-denoising comparisons.

Abstract

We develop a class of horseshoe-type shrinkage priors generated by ratios of independent numerator and denominator random variables. Large denominator values produce concentration near zero, small denominator values generate heavy tails, and the numerator determines scale and geometry and may also influence tail behavior. We establish general origin and tail asymptotics, including power and logarithmic singularities. Under a Gaussian likelihood and regularly varying prior tails, the posterior mean approaches the observation for large signals, with risk approaching that of the unshrunk observation. The denominator also induces a latent shrinkage factor. For a Cauchy denominator, this factor has exactly the ${\rm Beta}(1/2,1/2)$ distribution, whatever the numerator. With a Gaussian numerator and appropriate scale calibration, it recovers the classical horseshoe shrinkage coefficient, whereas non-Gaussian numerators can change the effective posterior shrinkage, even in its asymptotic rate. The Cauchy/Cauchy prior illustrates the theory, with a logarithmic singularity at the origin in one dimension, power singularities in higher dimensions, and logarithmically modified Cauchy-order tails. Its posterior mean shrinks smoothly, whereas its spatial median behaves nearly as a threshold rule. We also compare the Cauchy/Cauchy posterior mean with fifteen established shrinkage procedures on the four standard Donoho-Johnstone test functions, finding competitive, though not uniformly superior, wavelet-denoising performance. The ratio construction generalizes several published shrinkage priors and offers a flexible framework for sparse estimation, with natural applications to wavelet shrinkage, variable selection, and structured multivariate shrinkage.
