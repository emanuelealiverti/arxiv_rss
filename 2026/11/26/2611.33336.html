Addressing Missing Exposures in Causal Inference: A Practical Guide

low Mo Wang, Elizabeth A. Stuart, Keith Barnatchez · stat.ME · 26 Nov 2026
Compares multiply-robust, single-imputation, and multiple-imputation approaches to missing exposure bias in doubly robust causal estimation.

Abstract

Missing exposure data are a common challenge in epidemiologic studies that can complicate the identification and estimation of causal effects. Particularly for researchers constructing doubly-robust estimators, it can be unclear how to adapt standard methods such as augmented inverse probability weighting (AIPW) to account for missing exposures. This paper clarifies challenges posed by missing exposures, and discusses approaches available for doubly-robust estimation in these settings. We outline numerous mechanisms that can give rise to missing exposures in practice, and demonstrate why double-robustness alone does not protect complete-case AIPW from missing exposure bias. We highlight three broad approaches for addressing this bias: multiply-robust estimation, single imputation and multiple imputation. Using complete-data AIPW—the estimator one would construct in the absence of missing data—as a benchmark, we demonstrate how these approaches impute different components of the ideal complete-data estimator. We discuss practical implementation challenges, including whether outcome information should be used to impute exposures or predict exposure missingness. Through simulations, we illustrate the critical roles of the missingness mechanism, treatment effect heterogeneity and model specification on the performance of these approaches. We conclude with recommendations to help researchers choose an analysis strategy and inform modeling choices involved in their implementation.
