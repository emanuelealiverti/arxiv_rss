Unsupervised and explainable authorization of change point in multivariate industrial data for trustworthy system and process monitoring

low Justyna Witulska, Tomasz Barszcz, Agnieszka Wy{\l}omańska, Ireneusz Jab{\l}oński · stat.ME · 26 Nov 2026
CHASM provides scalable nonparametric significance testing and root-cause analysis for change points detected in multivariate industrial data.

Abstract

The paper introduces a Change point Hypothesis Assessment for Significance in Multivariate data (CHASM) - a novel non-parametric approach for evaluating the significance of change points detected in multivariate data, especially relevant for a trustworthy autonomous industrial system. The method is highly scalable, doesn’t require data labeling, and allows for customizable evaluation criteria, such as inter-component relationships. Its non-parametric nature broadens applicability to non-Gaussian distributed data. CHASM incorporates robust auxiliary statistics that maintain reliability even in the presence of outliers, enabling accurate assessment of change point significance for complex data. Beyond statistical significance testing, CHASM also facilitates root-cause analysis of detected changes. It offers a solution expected by practitioners for validating change points identified by segmentation-based algorithms, particularly those lacking rigorous statistical foundations. To support situation aware decision making, a {trustworthy factor} is introduced and its performance demonstrated for simulated and real-world data recorded in machine monitoring system. Using MAPE, it was evidenced that the two-stage procedure - identification of the change point and authorization of the change point - significantly outperforms (23-fold in the reported experiment) the one-stage scheme. All these are essential in real-world applications, where overfitting and false alarms can lead to loss of trust in technical solutions.
