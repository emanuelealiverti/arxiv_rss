A Spatial Analysis of Proximity to the Built Environment and Wildfire Ignition Cause in the United States

low Ardavan Yazdanbakhsh · stat.AP · 16 Nov 2026
The paper classifies wildfire ignitions using building proximity to predict human versus lightning cause.

Abstract

Background. Human activities in areas with burnable land cover can lead to wildfire ignitions, and higher levels of human presence increase the likelihood of such fires. Building locations provide a widely available representation of human presence. Aims. This study examines how accurately proximity to buildings predicts wildfire ignition cause across different land-cover types. Methods. Supervised classification methods were applied to building, land-cover, and ignition data for the contiguous United States to estimate the conditional probability that an ignition is human-caused. Proximity to buildings was represented using either building density within surrounding zones or distance to nearby areas with high building density. Key results. Both methods, particularly the distance-based approach, showed that proximity to buildings is a strong predictor of ignition cause: approximately three quarters of both human- and lightning-caused ignitions were correctly classified. Retraining the models separately for individual states did not substantially improve classification performance relative to the national model. Conclusions. Building proximity is an effective predictor of wildfire ignition cause, and the relationship between ignition cause and building-related predictors is similar across many regions of the contiguous United States. Implications. Building-proximity variables can be incorporated into wildfire forecasting and risk assessment models to improve prediction of human-caused wildfire ignitions.
