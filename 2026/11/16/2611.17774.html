Estimand-specific bootstrap confidence intervals after optimism correction for prediction models

low Seiya Takano, Kazuki Nishida, Ryo Emoto, Shigeyuki Matsui · stat.ME · 16 Nov 2026

Abstract

Confidence intervals after bootstrap optimism correction are often reported for prediction models, but what they cover depends on the estimand. We distinguish two estimands: the target-population performance of the final model developed from the observed sample, $\theta_{\mathrm{FM},n}$, and the average performance over repeated model development, $\theta_{\mathrm{proc},n}$. Using first-order expansions of the estimation errors, we identify the bootstrap root that matches the error for $\theta_{\mathrm{FM},n}$. We use this root in the nested error bootstrap (NEB) as a new reference method, and we show that the optimism error bootstrap (OEB), which needs no inner bootstrap, approximates NEB. We also show that the conventional two-stage bootstrap (TSB) and location-shifted bootstrap (LSB) reproduce the first-order variation for $\theta_{\mathrm{proc},n}$. In simulations, NEB generally achieved coverage closer to the nominal level than TSB and LSB for the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve, Brier score, and log loss. OEB was close to NEB at moderate to high events per variable (EPV) but could give narrower intervals at low EPV. The distinction is particularly clear for calibration measures: first-order effects of perturbing the training and evaluation distributions cancel in the TSB root, yielding a degenerate first-order limit. In contrast, the NEB root has a nondegenerate first-order limit and yields asymptotically valid confidence intervals for $\theta_{\mathrm{FM},n}$ under regularity conditions. Consistent with theory, TSB showed substantial undercoverage for calibration even at larger sample sizes. Our findings emphasize the need to construct confidence intervals for a specified estimand and performance measure.
