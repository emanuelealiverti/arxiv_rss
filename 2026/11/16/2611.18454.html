Dynamic bigraphical Matérn--Whittle processes: learning dependence graphs among spatial-temporal fields

high Alokesh Manna, Debangan Dey · stat.ME, stat.ML · 16 Nov 2026
Introduces dynamic bigraphical Matérn–Whittle processes for time‑varying dependence graphs among spatial‑temporal fields.

Abstract

Many studies record several variables at the same sites over a sequence of periods, such as pollutant concentrations at monitoring stations or case counts of infectious diseases by district. A central question concerns the dependence between the variables and its change over time. We develop dynamic bigraphical Mat'ern–Whittle (DBMW) processes for such data. Each variable has a spatial field that evolves by autoregression. A graph over the variables couples the temporal innovations of the fields. Its links appear and vanish over time, and a link may change sign. A graph Laplacian on the sites gives the spatial structure, and a canonical exponential family links the fields to Gaussian, count or binomial responses. The joint distribution of the latent fields over all sites, variables and periods factorizes over the eigenmodes of the graph Laplacian, and its normalizing constant is free of the graph and of the dynamics. Given the fields, the posterior of the time-varying signed graph is therefore exact, by forward–backward recursions. The estimator has three steps: a fit without a graph, which also gives the switching rates with an interval, the exact graph posterior, and a joint refit. In simulations, DBMW recovers the graph with higher average precision than graphical lasso, time-varying graphical lasso and Poisson log-normal networks, and it predicts withheld data better than a standard spatio-temporal model that fits each variable separately, with a Mat'ern field and autoregressive evolution, by integrated nested Laplace approximation. In weekly air-quality data from 288 U.S. sites, it recovers the seasonal change of sign between ozone and nitrogen dioxide. In German surveillance data on seven diseases, the district-level links stay positive and stable through the COVID-19 pandemic.
