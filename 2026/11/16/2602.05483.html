Toward Operationalizing Rasmussen in Evolving Software Systems

low Anatoly A. Krasnovsky · stat.AP · 16 Nov 2026
Proposes a runtime observer using measurement history and MEWMA to quantify software drift and detect resource redistribution.

Abstract

Rasmussen’s qualitative drift model explains how successive, locally successful adaptations under efficiency pressures can move a system toward unacceptable operation. Brooker and Hochstein applied this perspective conceptually to software operations without specifying a quantitative procedure for observing drift. We take a step in this direction and propose a runtime observer that quantitatively operationalizes the model’s observable part for evolving software. The proposed observer is intended to give engineers evidence for pausing or revising adaptation before declared operating limits are crossed. It tracks how activity or resource use is distributed across components and types of work. Measurement history quantifies accumulated change and recent movement toward or away from current limits. Assuming that direction and limits remain unchanged, the proposed observer calculates the first constraint ahead and the amount of further change required to reach it. Measurements remain comparable across supported component changes, while policy revisions are distinguished from changes in execution. Preliminary runtime studies demonstrated these distinctions under annotated software changes and revised retry limits. A separate multivariate exponentially weighted moving average (MEWMA) experiment detected CPU redistribution earlier with log-ratio coordinates than with raw shares, motivating investigation of representation-dependent observation. Further work will ground constraints in service outcomes, investigate runtime-model acquisition, and test whether decisions informed by observed drift reduce violations at acceptable useful-work loss and intervention cost.
