Hybrid Time Series Models for Short-Term Sea-Level Forecasting

low Pierdomenico Duttilo, Francesco Lisi · stat.AP · 20 Nov 2026
Hybrid sea-level forecasts combine harmonic analysis with data-driven models for hourly tide predictions.

Abstract

Accurate tide forecasts are essential for coastal management, navigation, flood-risk reduction, and infrastructure protection. Observed sea level can be decomposed into astronomical and non-astronomical components, the latter mainly driven by meteorological effects. This study investigates a hybrid framework for hourly sea-level forecasting that combines harmonic analysis (HA) for the astronomical component with data-driven models for the non-astronomical contribution. The approach is evaluated at six tide-gauge stations with different tidal regimes: Venice, Trieste, Saint-Malo, Vard{\o}, Nikiski, and Nagasaki. Four data-driven model classes are considered: (i) autoregressive and (ii) functional autoregressive models with exogenous variables; (iii) generalized additive models and autoregressive neural networks; and (iv) a novel semi-functional k-nearest-neighbours approach that combines similarity in recent non-astronomical trajectories and meteorological conditions. Point forecasts are complemented by upper quantile forecasts, obtained by historical simulation of out-of-sample errors, targeting the right tail of the sea-level distribution. At the 24-hour horizon, hybrid models reduce the mean absolute and root mean squared errors of HA by 54.9% and 52.9% on average, while the best hybrid model reduces the HA quantile score by 40–60%. HA systematically underestimates the right tail. No hybrid specification dominates across locations, although the generalized additive model is the most competitive overall. For Venice, replacing observed meteorological covariates with operational Global Forecast System weather forecasts leaves 24- and 48-hour accuracy virtually unchanged. A cost–loss analysis of the MoSE flood-barrier system, based on these real-time forecasts, shows that hybrid forecasts substantially reduce decision losses relative to HA.
