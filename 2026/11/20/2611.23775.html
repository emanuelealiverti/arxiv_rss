Anytime-valid goodness-of-fit test by $e$-spacings

medium Yunda Hao, Kin Wai Chan · stat.ME, stat.TH · 20 Nov 2026
Constructs spacing-based e-processes for anytime-valid nonparametric goodness-of-fit testing.

Abstract

To perform an anytime-valid goodness-of-fit test, we propose spacing-based (e)-processes that control the type-I error rate under arbitrary stopping rules. Based on the maximum spacing of order statistics, we construct spacing-based (e)-processes for nonparametrically testing distribution. The resulting procedures are simple and computationally efficient, and are power-consistent. We call them $e$-spacing tests. This approach is especially powerful in detecting localized discrepancy between the data-generating density and the null density. Under mild conditions, the logarithm of the proposed (e)-process is asymptotically equivalent to that of the oracle-optimal (e)-process. We further generalize the construction from maximal spacing to maximum spacing set in the form of the (1/k) fraction of the largest (m)th order spacings, as a measure of the lower-level set of density. It boosts power under more global discrepancy from the null. Furthermore, we propose a greedy predictable scheme, via argmax or softmax rules, to combine multiple $e$-spacing testing strategies. We prove that it optimally tunes the $e$-spacing test. The proposed approach is readily generalizable to multivariate testing via a coordinate-wise Rosenblatt-transform. Simulation studies demonstrate that the proposed methods perform favorably relative to the considered anytime-valid competitors as well as various types of multiple test-adjusted Kolmogorov–Smirnov tests.
