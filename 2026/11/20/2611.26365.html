Interpreting the Morbidity Gap

low Alec Morton · stat.AP · 20 Nov 2026
Shows stochastic dominance conditions linking morbidity gap to disability rates and mortality.

Abstract

The Global Burden of Disease (GBD) study now reports the morbidity gap (MG), life expectancy minus healthy life expectancy, as an indicator in its own right. Writing MG as the expected cumulative rate of years lived with disability (YLDs) at the life-table age at death, we use stochastic dominance results to show what MG measures, how it responds to mortality and how it relates to the YLDs of the current population. We show the following. (a) A YLD profile gives an MG no larger than another’s under every life table if and only if its cumulative YLD rate is nowhere higher. (b) With YLD rates fixed, lower mortality at every age widens MG, so that removing a risk factor that raises mortality reduces MG by less than its contribution to MG in GBD’s risk factor attribution. (c) A family of results gives easily interpretable conditions on the age structure of the population for the crude YLD rate to be above or below the life-table YLD rate, which is MG divided by life expectancy, and these conditions change with population ageing. We illustrate the results for Singapore, where MG widened from 8.9 to 11.2 years between 1990 and 2023.
