Bayesian Nonparametric Survival Regression with Time-Varying Covariates and Random Effects

high Kemin Zhang, Yudan Zou, Fan Li, Liangyuan Hu · stat.ME · 20 Nov 2026
Introduces a Bayesian nonparametric survival model with time‑varying covariates and random effects using tree‑based additive regression.

Abstract

In longitudinal survival studies, biomarkers and treatments change across visits, their associations with event risk may be nonlinear and change over time, and subjects with identical measurements may differ in risk. Existing tree-ensemble survival models address these features separately: discrete-time formulations accommodate covariate updates without subject-specific random effects, and hazard-based ensembles with frailty take covariates as fixed at baseline. We propose Time-Varying Survival Bayesian Additive Regression Trees (TVSurvBART), a continuous-time hazard model combining a flexible baseline, a log-linear tree ensemble, and subject-specific random intercepts and slopes; covariates enter as predictable step processes, and interval-start time among the tree inputs lets effects change across visits. Integrated exposure offsets absorb the within-interval variation of the baseline and random slopes, so the tree leaves retain conjugate updates. Re-centering moves along likelihood-invariant directions and alternating centered and non-centered covariance updates address the resulting posterior dependence. In two simulation families, TVSurvBART’s landmark predictions from held-out subjects’ histories up to the landmark time have higher time-dependent AUC than the Cox, frailty Cox, and forest benchmarks in every setting and lower Brier scores in all but one comparison, although population-summary gains are not uniform. In the CARRA juvenile idiopathic arthritis registry, discrimination for recorded flares is similar to Cox regression, while a secondary interval-probability score favors TVSurvBART.
