Spatiotemporal Risk-Based Hotspot Dynamics Assessment

medium José M. Angulo, Ana E. Madrid · stat.ME · 20 Nov 2026
Introduces a spatiotemporal risk-based hotspot framework using conditional random fields and sliding‑window empirical distributions.

Abstract

Analysis of hotspots often constitutes the final objective in application studies oriented to spatiotemporal risk assessment, particularly for risk mapping and as a helpful instrument for decision making, in diverse fields such as Ecology, Epidemiology, Environmental Sciences, Security, among others. The most primary form, common in practice, consists in identifying and depicting the areas where the magnitude of interest exceeds a certain critical threshold level. In this work, we introduce a wider, more general concept of `hotspot’, associated with the extremal values of risk measures defined on different structural indicators of threshold exceedance sets, such as the exceedance area, the excess volume, etc. In particular, we propose a methodological approach based on empirical characterization of the indicator probability distributions from simulation conditional on the available observations, calculated at local resolution scales determined by sliding windows. Risk map generation is then supported by a proper conceptualization and formalization of a suitable family of risk conditional random fields (RCRFs), specified in terms of a set of input elements that can be viewed as flexible tuning parameters for practical implementation and application. For illustration, we consider an extension of a spatiotemporal diffusive blur-generated autoregressive model involving a dynamical spatial deformation. Evolutionary assessment of hotspots is then addressed under different scenarios, with reference to well-known quantile-based risk measures.
