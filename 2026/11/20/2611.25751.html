Matrix elliptical sliced inverse regression: structured dimension reduction for heavy-tailed data

low Dingzi Guo, Wenhui Li, Yalin Wang, Wang Zhou · stat.ME · 20 Nov 2026

Abstract

Sliced inverse regression is widely used for sufficient dimension reduction, but existing matrix extensions that rely on covariance-based statistics can be unstable under heavy-tailed distributions. We propose matrix elliptical sliced inverse regression (MESIR) for estimating response-relevant row and column reduction subspaces under matrix elliptical distributions. We establish a mode-wise linearity relation linking the inverse regression curve to these subspaces through the corresponding scatter matrices. MESIR combines inverse-regression Kendall’s tau matrices with eigenvalue-calibrated marginal Kendall’s tau matrices. The calibration recovers the row and column scatter matrices needed to identify the reduction subspaces. Theoretically, we derive convergence rates for the calibrated marginal and inverse-regression estimators and establish subspace consistency under weak moment conditions, allowing the matrix dimensions and number of slices to diverge with the sample size. We further develop a consistent eigenvalue-ratio procedure for structural dimension selection and extend the method and theory to higher-order tensor predictors. Thorough simulation studies are conducted to show competitive performance under light tails and increasingly pronounced gains as the tails become heavier. Applications to COVID-19 CT images and radar target recognition demonstrate favorable empirical performance.
