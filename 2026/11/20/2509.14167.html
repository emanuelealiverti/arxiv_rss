Deconstructing Intraocular Pressure: A Non-Invasive Multi-Stage Probabilistic Inverse Framework

medium Md Rezwan Jaher, Abul Mukid Mohammad Mukaddes, A. B. M. Abdul Malek · stat.AP, stat.ME · 20 Nov 2026
Introduces a two-stage physics‑guided probabilistic inverse framework for deconvolving intraocular pressure data.

Abstract

We propose the mechanistically hierarchical inverse problem (MHIP) paradigm for biological transport systems, where a macroscopic observation emerges from the multiplicative entanglement of intrinsic micro-scale physics and latent macro-scale geometry. Without in vivo ground truth, solving MHIPs from clinical data is severely underdetermined. Standard statistical sampling degenerates along non-identifiable manifolds, while monolithic machine learning baselines collapse to an uninterpretable conditional mean. We propose a two-stage physics-guided decomposition that turns a globally non-identifiable inverse mapping into conditionally identifiable sub-problems. The first stage is a constrained inverse surrogate that isolates the intrinsic parameter under a fixed reference geometry. The second stage performs empirical structural calibration against population-level clinical data. We also introduce Physics-Calibrated Data Scaling (PCDS), a multi-fidelity strategy that builds large training sets from few high-fidelity simulations without patient-specific 3D modeling. In a glaucoma case study, the framework deconstructs a single intraocular pressure (IOP) measurement to estimate trabecular permeability ($\hat{K}{TM}$) and conventional outflow facility ($\hat{C}{trab}$). In retrospective validation against published in vivo data, the non-invasive $\hat{C}{trab}$ estimates showed low bias against measured outflow facility. In an ablation over 27 clinical cohorts, naive monolithic baselines reach 37.0% diagnostic accuracy. Adding the isolated $\hat{K}{TM}$ estimate raises this to 85.2%. The full two-stage framework reaches 88.9%. The framework also returns a probabilistic profile of the latent hydrodynamic state, offering a general computational blueprint for MHIPs.
