The optimal sub-Gaussian normalisation for randomised monotone functions

low Thomas Anton, Rabee Tourky · stat.ME · 16 Jun 2026
This paper provides a theoretical result on the optimal sub-Gaussian normalisation for randomised monotone functions, which is a topic in statistical learning theory, but not directly related to the researcher's core interests in Bayesian methodology and computation.

Abstract

Let $\mathcal{M}$ denote the class of randomised monotone functions on $\mathbb{R}$ with values in $[0,1]$, and let $U_{\mathcal{M}}\colon \mathbb{R}+\to \mathbb{R}+$ be the minimal function for which $$ \mathbb{P}\left{ \sqrt{\eta_f}\, \sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}} \left f_Z(t) - \Exf{f_Z(t)} \right \ge \varepsilon\sqrt{U_{\mathcal{M}}(\eta_f)} \right} \le 2\e^{-2\varepsilon^2} \(holds for every member $f_Z$ of $\mathcal{M}$ with finite effective sample size $\eta_f$ and every positive $\varepsilon$. We prove that for every $x> 1$,\) \left \sqrt{U_{\mathcal{M}}(x)} - \sqrt{\log_4 x} \right \le 2 \min!\left{ 1,\, \frac{2 \ln(\e + \ln x)}{\sqrt{\ln x}} \right}\,. $$ The optimal adjustment $\sqrt{U_{\mathcal{M}}(x)}$ matches $\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\ln 2}}\sqrt{\ln x}$ for all $x>1$, with residuals bounded as above.
