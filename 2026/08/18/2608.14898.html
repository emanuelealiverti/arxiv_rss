Sequential Importance Sampling for Thinned Count Autoregressions via Latent Gaussian Transformations

medium Joey Fingold, Justin J. Slater · stat.ME, stat.AP · 18 Aug 2026
The paper proposes a sequential importance sampling procedure to correct misspecified models.

Abstract

Thinned count autoregressions are popular for modelling infectious disease surveillance data due to their flexibility and interpretability. However, such a model is challenging to fit since it involves high-dimensional and serially correlated integer-valued unknowns. One solution is to consider an analogous continuous-valued surrogate model whose values are post-hoc mapped to integers. This procedure produces biased estimates as inference is performed using samples from such a surrogate model and not the thinned count autoregression itself. In this work, we propose a sequential importance sampling procedure to correct this misspecified model. We demonstrate its validity in a simulation study and its applicability for epidemic curve reconstruction using rotavirus data from Germany and meningococcus data from France.
